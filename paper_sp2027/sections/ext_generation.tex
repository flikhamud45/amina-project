\subsection{Generated Responses as One Prefill: Details and Full Measurements}
\label{sec:ext-gen}

\para{Per-prefix queries.} Proving a response of $G$ tokens as one query per prefix repeats the
prompt in every query: the $i$-th query has $P+i-1$ positions, so the claims cover
$\sum_{i=1}^{G}(P+i-1)=GP+G(G-1)/2$ positions, and mode $\sC$ needs $G$ column openings. The single
prefill of Section~\ref{sec:gen} has $P+G-1$ positions and one column opening per response. For
Llama-2-7B with $P=G=512$, the mode-$\sC$ proofs of the prefixes would take about \GenPerPrefixTB{}\,TB
(estimated from the measured claims per position at 64 and 2{,}048 tokens plus the fixed part of a
query), against \GenPrefillGB{}\,GB measured for the prefill; the same estimate is within
\GenPrefillModelWithin{}\% of the prefill's measured size. The prefill takes \GenPrefillProveC{}\,s to
prove and \GenPrefillVerifyC{}\,s to verify.

\para{The graph and the token rule.} Every slice of the decoder that keeps only the last position
(the one before the LM head, and those of the pruned last block) keeps the last $G$ positions
instead. Operations, weights and multipliers are those of the original graph; the number of LM-head
positions enters the graph digest that Fiat--Shamir absorbs (Section~\ref{sec:fs}), and the tokens
are part of the input, which the statement absorbs. The token rule costs one argmax over $V\times G$ values.
The rest of the query is that of one prefill: the claims of all $T$ positions, one Freivalds
identity per matrix over $T$ columns, one vector $\chi$ and one column opening per response in mode
$\sC$, and the range check. In $\Kpre$ the token rule can run online as claims arrive.

\para{Soundness, in detail.} Suppose some $y_i$ is not the greedy token. The verifier computes the
argmax from the claimed logits at position $P+i-1$, so the token rule passes only if those claimed
logits differ from the model's logits on the same prefix, so some claim is wrong. The verifier
recomputes every operation without weights and rejects a wrong claim of one, so the first wrong claim
that can pass is that of a weight operation whose input is correct. Freivalds' check then fails except with probability $p^{-r}$ per matrix, or
the column check of mode $\sC$ fails except with its column term (Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}).

\para{Tests.} Tests on four small decoders (GPT-, Llama-, OPT- and Qwen-style) confirm that the
logits at the last $G$ positions equal those of the separate prefix queries, and that a wrong
token, or logits forged to select it, is rejected by the token rule or by the LM head's check.

\para{Full measurements.} Table~\ref{tab:gen} gives the greedy responses in both modes, and
Table~\ref{tab:gen-full} adds sampled responses and the proof per generated token. Each cell has 3
timed queries, all accepted, and both tampered queries (a wrong token, and logits forged to choose
it) were rejected in every cell. In mode $\sC$, a greedy 256-token GPT-2 response after a 64-token
prompt takes \GenGptProveC{}\,s to prove. The proof per generated token of Llama-2-7B in mode $\sC$
falls from \GenLlamaMBPerTokenShort{}\,MB at $G=64$ to \GenLlamaMBPerTokenLong{}\,MB at $G=256$,
because the prompt's claims and the opened columns are shared by all the tokens of a response.

\para{Against unverified generation.} Proving a response takes time of the same order as generating
it. The provider generates the response before it proves it. Without a proof, Hugging Face
Transformers' fp16 decoders of the same checkpoints (scaled-dot-product attention, a KV cache,
greedy tokens) generate the 256-token responses after the 512-token prompts on the same GPU in
\GenDecodeLlama{}\,s (Llama-2-7B) and \GenDecodeQwen{}\,s (Qwen3-4B), medians of the timed responses.
For $G\in\{64,256\}$ the prover takes \GenOverDecodeC{} times the time of generating the same response
in mode $\sC$ and \GenOverDecodeK{} times in $\Kpre$.

\subsection{The Integer Sampler: Table, Truncation and Tests}
\label{sec:ext-sampling}

\para{The table.} The table $\Gamma$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gumbel-table} is a public constant whose
SHA-256 digest is fixed in the code. A platform whose logarithm rounds an entry differently
recomputes it in exact decimal arithmetic and checks the digest again.

\para{Truncation.} The end values are $\Gamma[0]=-161{,}658$ and $\Gamma[2^{16}-1]=772{,}243$, about
$-2.467\cdot2^{16}$ and $11.783\cdot2^{16}$. Let $z_{\max}$ be the largest entry of $z$, which is
always a candidate. Its score is at least $az_{\max}+\Gamma[0]$, and that of entry $v$ at most
$az_v+\Gamma[2^{16}-1]$. Hence $v$ is never
chosen if $a(z_{\max}-z_v)>933{,}901$, that is, if its temperature-scaled logit is more than about
$14.25$ below the largest. Under exact softmax sampling at temperature $\tau$, such a token has
probability at most $e^{-14.25}<6.5\cdot10^{-7}$. The total-variation distance to exact sampling on
real logits, which also reflects the quantisation of the uniform variables to steps of $2^{-16}$,
is measured in Section~\ref{sec:gen-sampling}.

\para{Tests.} Tests confirm that $k=1$ or a very low temperature gives greedy decoding, that flat
logits give near-uniform choices (each of 8 tokens chosen 380 to 620 times in 4{,}000 positions),
that sampled responses are accepted in modes $\sC$ and $\Kpre$ and with V1 (Section~\ref{sec:v1}),
and that the same tokens are rejected under another seed unless it happens to choose them.

\para{Stop rules and nucleus sampling.} Stop rules, such as an end-of-sequence token or a length
limit, need no further proof: the client checks them on the tokens that the token rule fixes. Nucleus
(top-$p$) sampling would need a cumulative sum of exponentials in exact integer arithmetic, which
we have not implemented.

\subsection{Quality of the Integer Model: Attribution, Smoothing and Published Results}
\label{sec:ext-quality}

\para{Attribution method.} We re-implemented the integer graph in floating point with each
component switchable between its exact integer form and float. With every component integer it
reproduces the integer graph's logits exactly, and with every component float it matches the
checkpoint's to within \AttrFloatTol{}. Table~\ref{tab:quality-attr} gives the leave-one-out
perplexity (that component in float, the rest integer) and the add-one perplexity (only that
component integer) of OPT-125M on 16 WikiText-2 windows of 128 tokens. Its integer graph has one
scalar norm gain of 4 and 8-bit probabilities and is calibrated on one 128-token window at the end
of the test split.

\para{Where the perplexity goes.} The LayerNorm outputs dominate: making them float removes more
than half of the gap (\AttrIntPpl{} to \AttrNormLoo{}, against \AttrFpPpl{} in fp32). The weight
matrices and the requantisation before fc1 follow, while the 8-bit softmax, the residual stream and
the ReLU table each add at most \AttrSmallCost{} to the perplexity on their own. The LayerNorm loss
comes from rounding, not clipping. A few outlier channels of OPT's residual stream, and a large
channel at the beginning-of-sequence token, inflate the standard deviation, so a scalar gain that
keeps them within int8 leaves the other channels few levels. The best scalar gains fall with the
width (4, 3 and 2 for OPT-125M, OPT-1.3B and OPT-6.7B), which is consistent with the larger loss of
the largest model.

\para{Smoothing, in detail.} In the gain $\gamma_j=\kappa/s_j$ of Section~\ref{sec:quality}, the
constant $\kappa$ maps the largest $\rho_j\gamma_j$ to 127, and $1/\gamma_j$ is folded into column $j$
of the matrices that the norm feeds before their per-matrix quantisation. Every requantisation
multiplier stays a scalar, and both proof formats accept a smoothed graph in our tests.

\para{Calibration statistics.} Each channel's maximum without clipping is stable across calibration
windows (OPT-1.3B within \QualityDrawSpread{} over two draws); our first recipe's percentiles were
not. That recipe took $\rho_j$ as the 99.9th percentile of the channel's normalised magnitude,
clipped the q/k/v, fc1 and attention-output requantisations at the 99.99th percentile, and used
$\alpha=0.55$. On 128-token windows calibrated on the test split it gave
$\times\LegacySmallLo{}$--$\LegacySmallHi{}$ on OPT-125M at $\alpha=0.55$ and at $\alpha=0.5$, but at
2{,}048 tokens with train-split calibration it transferred badly
($\times\QualityLegacyLo{}$--$\QualityLegacyHi{}$ on OPT-1.3B depending on the calibration windows,
about $\times$\QualityLegacyOptS{} on OPT-125M), and OPT-1.3B lost \ZeroshotLegacyMean{} points of
mean zero-shot accuracy (\ZeroshotMeanOptM{} with the certified recipe). The Llama recipe keeps
percentile statistics; over three draws of four train windows each (with the prefix chosen on the
first), its ratio moves by \LlamaSevenDrawSpread{} on Llama-2-7B, \TinyLlamaDrawSpread{} on TinyLlama
and \QwenMDrawSpread{} on Qwen3-1.7B (at most \LlamaDrawSpread{}).

\para{Choice of $\alpha$.} The certified recipe's
$\alpha=0.6$ was chosen on the test split: over $\alpha\in\{0.3,\dots,0.7\}$ its ratio is
\QualityAlphaGapOptS{} above the best on OPT-125M and \QualityAlphaGapOptM{} above it on OPT-1.3B. On the validation split, which
no choice used, it is \QualityAlphaValGapOptS{} and \QualityAlphaValGapOptM{} above the best of $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.6,0.7\}$ on
OPT-125M and OPT-1.3B, and costs OPT-6.7B \QualityAlphaCostOptL{} against $\alpha=0.5$. The Llama recipe's $\alpha$
and SwiGLU balance were likewise chosen per model on the test split, except Llama-2-13B's, fixed in
advance at ($\alpha=0.6$, balance 0.2). Llama-2-7B's started from ($\alpha=0.55$, balance 0.2), taken in advance from a licence-free model of its shapes; once its
prefix absorbed the massive activation, the best of
$\alpha\in\{0.45,0.55,0.65\}$ and balance $\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$ on the test split was \LlamaSevenTestBest{}, which we use (an edge of
the grid), and on the validation split it is \LlamaSevenValBest{}, ours \LlamaSevenValGap{} above it;
on Llama-2-13B the best of $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.6,0.7\}$ is \LlamaThirteenTestBest{} and \LlamaThirteenValBest{}, ours
\LlamaThirteenTestGap{} and \LlamaThirteenValGap{} above them. On the validation split, TinyLlama's setting is

against \CleWithout{} on OPT-125M at 128 tokens).

\para{Zero-shot accuracy.} Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot} scores the certified models on six tasks of the

\para{Per-row weight scales.} Per-row power-of-two weight scales (four per matrix) on top of
smoothing reach $\times$\pviLoHi{\RowScalesLo}{\RowScalesHi}{--} on OPT-125M at 128 tokens, with top-1 agreement
\pviLoHi{\RowScalesTopOneLo}{\RowScalesTopOneHi}{ to },
but do not help at 2{,}048 tokens. They give a requantisation four multipliers. v0 recomputes such a
requantisation like any other operation without weights, but V1's windows assume one multiplier per operation, so we do not adopt them; supporting a few
multipliers per operation in V1 would remove this restriction.

\para{Further published results.} ZeroQuant-V2's setting uses 8-bit per-token dynamic activations
and 16-bit weights~\cite{yao2023zeroquantv2}. In the evaluation of CushionCache, with group-wise
weight scales, static W8A8 costs OPT-6.7B 5.4\% and SmoothQuant-O3 nothing
measurable~\cite{son2024cushioncache}. Proof systems that keep 16-bit values stay under
1\%~\cite{zkllm,zkgpt}, whereas our range bound on the claims and the prover's exact float32
products assume int8 activations (Section~\ref{sec:intmodel}). SLP's fixed-point TinyLlama is 8.5\%
above its float model and agrees with it on 84.8\% of top-1 tokens~\cite{slp}.
