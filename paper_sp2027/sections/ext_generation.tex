\subsection{Generated Responses as One Prefill: Details and Full Measurements}
\label{sec:ext-gen}

\para{Per-prefix queries.} Proving a response of $G$ tokens as one query per prefix repeats the
prompt in every query: the $i$-th query has $P+i-1$ positions, so the claims cover
$\sum_{i=1}^{G}(P+i-1)=GP+G(G-1)/2$ positions, and mode $\sC$ needs $G$ column openings. The single
prefill of Section~\ref{sec:gen} has $P+G-1$ positions and one column opening per response. For
Llama-2-7B with $P=G=512$, this is about 1.3\,TB of claims against about 3.3\,GB.

\para{The graph and the token rule.} Every ``last position'' slice of the decoder (the one before
the LM head, and those of a pruned last block) takes the last $G$ positions instead. Operations,
weights and multipliers are those of the original graph, and the number of LM-head positions
enters the graph digest that Fiat--Shamir absorbs (Section~\ref{sec:fs}); the tokens are part of
the input $x$, which the statement absorbs. The token rule costs one argmax over $V\times G$ values.
The rest of the query is that of one prefill: the claims of all $T$ positions, one Freivalds
identity per matrix over $T$ columns, one vector $\chi$ and one column opening per response in mode
$\sC$, and the range check. In $\Kpre$ the token rule can run online as claims arrive.

\para{Soundness, in detail.} Suppose some $y_i$ is not the greedy token. The verifier computes the
argmax from the claimed logits at position $P+i-1$, so the token rule passes only if those claimed
logits differ from the model's logits on the same prefix. That is a wrong claim of the LM head or of
an earlier operation that feeds it. The first wrong weight operation has a correct input, which the
verifier recomputed, so Freivalds' check fails except with probability $p^{-r}$ per matrix, or the
column check of mode $\sC$ fails except with its column term (Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}).

\para{Tests.} Tests on four small decoders (GPT-, Llama-, OPT- and Qwen-style) confirm that the
logits at the last $G$ positions equal those of the separate prefix queries, and that a wrong
token, or logits forged to select it, is rejected by the token rule or by the LM head's check.

\para{Full measurements.} Table~\ref{tab:gen-full} extends Table~\ref{tab:gen} with $G=64$ and the
proof per generated token. Each cell has 3 queries, all accepted, and the tampered query was
rejected in every cell. In mode $\sC$, a 256-token GPT-2 response takes 0.29\,s to prove,
0.59\,s to verify and 87.3\,MB, against 0.15\,s, 0.21\,s and 14.9\,MB for one token. The proof per
generated token falls to 0.34\,MB for GPT-2 and 1.44\,MB for OPT-1.3B in mode $\sC$.

\subsection{The Integer Sampler: Table, Truncation and Tests}
\label{sec:ext-sampling}

\para{The table.} The table $\Gamma$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gumbel-table} is a public constant whose
SHA-256 digest is fixed in the code. A platform whose logarithm rounds an entry differently
recomputes it in exact decimal arithmetic and checks the digest again.

\para{Truncation.} The end values are $\Gamma[0]=-161{,}658$ and $\Gamma[2^{16}-1]=772{,}243$, about
$-2.467\cdot2^{16}$ and $11.783\cdot2^{16}$. Let $z_{\max}$ be the largest entry of $z$, which is
always a candidate. Its score is at least $az_{\max}+\Gamma[0]$, and that of entry $v$ at most
$az_v+\Gamma[2^{16}-1]$. Hence $v$ is never
chosen if $a(z_{\max}-z_v)>933{,}901$, that is, if its temperature-scaled logit is more than about
$14.25$ below the largest. Under exact softmax sampling at temperature $\tau$, such a token has
probability at most $e^{-14.25}<6.5\cdot10^{-7}$. The total-variation distance to exact sampling on
real logits, which also reflects the quantisation of the uniform variables to steps of $2^{-16}$,
is measured in Section~\ref{sec:gen-sampling}.

\para{Tests.} Tests confirm that $k=1$ or a very low temperature gives greedy decoding, that flat
logits give near-uniform choices (each of 8 tokens chosen 380 to 620 times in 4{,}000 positions),
that sampled responses are accepted in modes $\sC$ and $\Kpre$ and with V1 (Section~\ref{sec:v1}),
and that the same tokens are rejected under another seed unless it happens to choose them.

\para{Stop rules and nucleus sampling.} Stop rules, such as an end-of-sequence token or a length
limit, are properties of the response's length and need no proof beyond the token rule. Nucleus
(top-$p$) sampling would need a cumulative sum of exponentials in exact integer arithmetic, which
we have not implemented.

\subsection{Quality of the Integer Model: Attribution, Smoothing and Published Results}
\label{sec:ext-quality}

\para{Attribution method.} We re-implemented the integer graph in floating point with each
component switchable between its exact integer form and float. With every component integer it
reproduces the integer graph's logits exactly, and with every component float it matches the
checkpoint's to within $4\cdot10^{-5}$. Table~\ref{tab:quality-attr} gives the leave-one-out
perplexity (that component in float, the rest integer) and the add-one perplexity (only that
component integer) of OPT-125M on 16 WikiText-2 windows of 128 tokens.

\para{Where the perplexity goes.} The LayerNorm outputs dominate: making them float removes more
than half of the gap (75.5 to 69.4, against 64.8 in fp32). The weight matrices and the
requantisation before fc1 follow, while the 8-bit softmax, the residual stream and the ReLU table
each cost at most 0.1. The LayerNorm loss comes from rounding, not clipping. A few outlier channels
of OPT's residual stream, and a large channel at the beginning-of-sequence token, inflate the
standard deviation, so a scalar gain that keeps them within int8 leaves the other channels few
levels. The best scalar gains fall with the width (4, 3 and 2 for OPT-125M, OPT-1.3B and
OPT-6.7B), which is consistent with the larger loss of the largest model.

\para{Smoothing, in detail.} In the gain $\gamma_j=\kappa/s_j$ of Section~\ref{sec:quality}, the
constant $\kappa$ maps the largest $\rho_j\gamma_j$ to 127, and $1/\gamma_j$ is folded into column $j$
of the matrices that the norm feeds before their per-matrix quantisation. Every requantisation
multiplier stays a scalar, and both proof formats accept a smoothed graph in our tests. Our first
recipe took $\rho_j$ as the 99.9th percentile of the channel's normalised magnitude, clipped the
q/k/v, fc1 and attention-output requantisations at the 99.99th percentile, and used $\alpha=0.55$; on
128-token windows calibrated on the test split it gave $\times\LegacySmallLo{}$--$\LegacySmallHi{}$ on OPT-125M. At 2{,}048
tokens with train-split calibration these statistics transferred badly ($\times\QualityLegacyLo{}$--$\QualityLegacyHi{}$ on
OPT-1.3B depending on the calibration windows, about $\times2$ on OPT-125M), and the mean zero-shot
loss of OPT-1.3B was \ZeroshotLegacyMean{} points (\ZeroshotMeanOptM{} with the certified recipe below). Each channel's maximum without clipping (``smooth-max'') is
stable across calibration windows (OPT-1.3B $\times1.026$ for two draws); over $\alpha\in\{0.3,\dots,0.7\}$,
$\alpha=0.6$ is best on OPT-125M, ties $\alpha=0.7$ on OPT-1.3B and costs OPT-6.7B 0.002 against
$\alpha=0.5$, so it is the certified recipe. Cross-layer equalisation did not help on top of smoothing (\CleWith{} against \CleWithout{}
at 128 tokens).

\para{Zero-shot accuracy.} Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot} scores the certified models on six tasks of the
LM Evaluation Harness, re-implemented with its prompts and length normalisation (a fixed subset of
2{,}000 HellaSwag documents; every other task in full). Each integer model is compared with the same
checkpoint in fp16 on the same documents, with paired bootstrap intervals. The fp16 accuracies
agree with the harness's results for these checkpoints released with Pythia (its Table~16 and result
files)~\cite{biderman2023pythia} within 0.3 points on the five tasks scored in full (at most three
documents), and on the HellaSwag subset within twice its sampling error of the full-set values
of~\cite{dey2023cerebrasgpt,gu2023mamba}. The loss
falls with the model's size, as its perplexity ratio does, and is concentrated on LAMBADA, which
scores exact next-token predictions; the integer model picks the fp16 answer on \ZeroshotSamePick{}\% of the
documents.

\para{Per-row weight scales.} Per-row power-of-two weight scales (four per matrix) on top of
smoothing reach $\times\RowScalesLo{}$--$\RowScalesHi{}$ on OPT-125M at 128 tokens, with top-1 agreement \RowScalesTopOneLo{} to \RowScalesTopOneHi{},
but do not help at 2{,}048 tokens. They give a
requantisation four multipliers. v0 handles these as any other operation without weights,
but V1's windows assume one multiplier per operation, so we do not adopt them; supporting a few
multipliers per operation in V1 would remove this restriction.

\para{Further published results.} ZeroQuant-V2's setting uses 8-bit per-token dynamic activations
and 16-bit weights~\cite{yao2023zeroquantv2}. In the evaluation of CushionCache, with group-wise
weight scales, static W8A8 costs OPT-6.7B 5.4\% and SmoothQuant-O3 nothing
measurable~\cite{son2024cushioncache}. Proof systems that keep 16-bit values stay under
1\%~\cite{zkllm,zkgpt}, whereas our range bound on the claims and the prover's exact float32
products assume int8 activations (Section~\ref{sec:intmodel}). SLP's fixed-point TinyLlama is 8.5\%
above its float model and agrees with it on 84.8\% of top-1 tokens~\cite{slp}. Qwen3-4B is not
yet measured.
