\subsection{The Verifier Model: Remarks}
\label{ext:attack-model}

\para{What the verifier is given.} Definition~\ref{def:spot} gives the verifier more than any
deployed scheme has. It knows the query, every layer other than the target layer, public side
information $Z$ such as each neuron's natural range, and the entire claimed trace $\tau'$. Only the
rows of the target layer are behind the commitment oracle. A row-wise commitment, as in the path
protocol, has locality $d=1$ as long as its openings reveal nothing about unopened rows
(Remark~\ref{rem:merkle}). A column of the Reed--Solomon commitment of Section~\ref{sec:defence},
or a random combination $\chi^{\top}\!A$ of the layer's rows, has support $[N]$. In the target
layer, $s$ paths make $s$ queries of locality~$1$.

\para{Prover messages and succinct arguments.} The model treats every prover message that the
verifier checks against the commitment as an oracle query. Its support is the set of rows on which
the honest message depends. This is what the lower bound needs: the adversary answers every
message as the honest prover of the explaining instance $\theta'$ would, and a message whose
support avoids the edited row $u$ is then the same under $\theta$ and under $\theta'$. A folded row
$\chi^{\top}\!A$ depends on all rows, and so does the proof of a succinct argument about the layer.
A verifier that checks such a proof therefore makes a query of support $[N]$. It lies in
$\mathrm{Spot}_N(q)$, where the guessing bound $G_{qd}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:guess} equals $1$ as
soon as $qd\ge N$. This
is the sense in which SNARK-assisted verifiers are outside the model: the lower bound says nothing
about them, and part~(b) of Theorem~\ref{thm:dich} shows that queries of support $[N]$ can indeed
reach soundness $2^{-\lambda}$.

\para{Why recomputation matters.} The edited trace is the honest trace of an admissible model that
differs from the true one in row $u$ alone. Without recomputation, every child of $u$ would be
inconsistent, and an adaptive verifier could follow the inconsistencies back to $u$. With it,
opening rows only confirms that they are clean, and adaptivity adds nothing beyond a good guess.
This is the classical result on searching for a hidden object~\cite{chew1967,kadane1971}, with a
clean opened row in the role of an empty box. What is specific to proofs of inference is that
honest recomputation makes it apply to verifiers that read the whole trace.

\para{What reading the values buys.} With independent rows, the posterior on the edited neuron is
proportional to the adversary's propensity to choose it, divided by the prior probability of the
value written there (Corollary~\ref{cor:surprise}). A value-aware sampler therefore gains on edits
that write improbable values. It gains nothing on edits that write common ones, provided the
adversary's choice of neuron is itself spread out. Our measurements are consistent with this but
do not test it directly. The contribution-weighted sampler detects 9.2\% of the best single-neuron
edits and none of the zeroing edits. For the latter the reason is mechanical, since it never visits
a neuron whose claimed activation is zero (Section~\ref{sec:break}).

\para{The two regimes.} Opening $s$ distinct neurons uniformly reaches $s/N$ on every dense
network, so in the worst case nothing beats uniform sampling. Reaching $2^{-40}$ then takes about
$27.7N$ paths per query ($40\ln2\approx27.7$), which is the cost the path protocol was designed to
avoid. The two regimes of Theorem~\ref{thm:dich} differ by a factor $O(\lambda)$ in $qd$, and in
both the verifier touches every row. Mixing lowers the cost of each touched row from a full row of
weights to $O(\lambda)$ field elements. Part~(a) is the one-sided randomised query complexity of
$\mathrm{OR}_N$ placed inside a network, and the spot-check calculation behind it is folklore
(Section~\ref{sec:related}). Because the trace is free to read, the object that must be encoded or
folded is the model, not the trace. Our defence encodes only the model, which is why its proof
still grows with the trace.

\para{The worst-case instance.} With a hiding (salted) commitment the instance of
Proposition~\ref{prop:worst} needs no further ingredient. For an unsalted Merkle tree, random
weights on inputs that are always zero keep each opening from revealing other rows, at an additive
cost in the hash length (Remark~\ref{rem:merkle}).

\subsection{Measured Attacks: Details}
\label{ext:attack-measure}

\para{Finding the edit.} To choose the neuron $u$, the adversary ranks neurons by the gradient of
the decision margin. It then bisects for the smallest change of $a_u$ that makes the output wrong,
or equal to the class of its choice. The ranking uses only the later weights, so the attack depends
on the target rows only through their values, as Lemma~\ref{lem:product} requires.

\para{Reproduction.} Our implementation behaves as Anchuri et al.\ describe. On MNIST versions of
four of their substitute-model settings, honest provers passed all 3{,}000 runs of each, and
substitute models were caught in 99.8--100\% of runs, including an 8-bit quantised copy that agrees
with $M$ on all 150 evaluated queries. Without the $10^{-4}$ tolerance, an honest prover passes only
21--37\% of runs, because a layer-wide matrix product and a single-neuron dot product round
differently. The forgeries that Anchuri et al.\ consider are their substitute models (Appendices~E
and~F and Section~7.2 of~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}); on the MLP they corrupt hundreds of its 778
trace nodes.

\para{Other samplers on MNIST.} Against an adversary who knows the rule, a static importance score
(outgoing weight magnitude) detects 0.11\% of attacks and gradient saliency 0.016\%, against 0.20\%
for uniform sampling. The zeroing attack against the contribution-weighted sampler edits about 14
of the 512 neurons, whose most common value is zero (65\% of inputs). The floor $\delta=1$ detects
10$\times$ as often as uniform sampling; other floors give 0.9--6$\times$. Each figure is the
detection of the best attack we found against that sampler. The 9.2\% is one sampler against the
plain attack; the 2\% of the floor $\delta=1$ is the highest detection among our samplers, each
against the attacks built for it.

\para{Larger models.} A change to the least-visited penultimate neuron of a CNN is caught by one
path with probability as low as 1/28 million. The 305{,}193 paths for Llama-2-7B use its FFN width
11{,}008 and the fp16 weights of~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}.

\para{OPT-6.7B.} The search ran over the FFN neurons of layers 8, 16, 24 and 31 at the last
position. Because of the final LayerNorm, a last-layer neuron can force only its \emph{saturation
token}, so the 16{,}384 neurons of that layer reach 6{,}187 of the 50{,}272 tokens. This is why the
fixed-target backdoor succeeds for ``\texttt{back}'' and fails for ``\texttt{hacked}''. Under the
path protocol, detection depends only on how many sites are edited, not on what the edit does;
Table~\ref{tab:aligned} gives the paths needed for $k$ edited neurons in two instruction-tuned
models.

\subsection{Systematisation: Full Entries and Derivations}
\label{ext:attack-sok}

\para{Entries shortened in Table~\ref{tab:sok}.} The path protocol's authors note the $1/N$ limit
for one changed node, state that their other-model analysis ``trivially extends to full
soundness'' for binary-output models, and leave full soundness open for LLMs; their other-model
theorem is unaffected. The $2^{-40}$ figures are 14{,}182 paths for our dog-vs-cat ResNet-18 and at
least 305{,}193 and 454{,}248 for Llama-2-7B and OPT-6.7B. For SLP's TinyLlama configuration (5 of
45 chunks) one audit accepts the edit with probability 88.9\%. CommitLLM's routine audit covers
Llama-70B; by the authors' own finding an edited attention output passes every tier, and attention
is audited, not verified. NanoZK presents its audit mode as an efficiency tool, not a security
claim; its full mode checks every layer. SPEX states that sampling ``cannot prevent an adversarial
solver''. For TOPLOC and SVIP, token choice is out of scope. TensorCommitments states soundness
$\mathrm{negl}(\lambda)$ against any PPT prover as the goal of the problem, and no theorem claims
it; the gap comes from the predictable, weight-only layer selection, not from the commitment. The
IMMACULATE entry is our derivation from its flagging rule, for a response of $T$ steps.

\para{What the table shows.} We say that a claim exceeds the sampling only where a stated guarantee
is stronger than what the scheme's own sampling gives. An edit followed by honest recomputation is
caught exactly when the sample covers it: $s/N$ for paths, $3/161$ for SLP's 70B configuration,
one layer in eight for CommitLLM's routine audit, $|S|/L$ for NanoZK's audit mode, and never outside
the weight-determined layer selection of TensorCommitments. More generally, a selection by value or
by a public ranking gives an adaptive provider a place to hide. A deterministic choice is avoided
with certainty, and value-dependent choices have been evaded by edits that write common values,
both by our zeroing attack and by the blindfolded updates against
Proof-of-Learning~\cite{fang2023pol}. TOPLOC and SVIP target model substitution, and a forged
answer with honestly recomputed fingerprints lies outside what they claim.

\subsection{Disclosure}
\label{ext:attack-disclosure}

For each notified scheme we described what we found, under which of its stated parameters, and what
we do not claim, and we offered our draft and code. Where a scheme states a probabilistic
guarantee, we report the acceptance probability that follows from its own formulas and do not call

\subsection{The Lower Bound: Further Remarks}
\label{ext:attack-bound}

\para{The reference oracle.} The expected number of queries $q$ in Definition~\ref{def:spot} is
measured under the reference oracle $R_p$ of Definition~\ref{def:H}. That oracle matches the real
run until the edited neuron is touched, and this is all that the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:guess}
uses: detection requires a touch of the edited row, and the probability of a touch is fixed by the
run up to the choice of that query.

\para{The posterior.} With independent rows, the posterior on the edited neuron is proportional to
its propensity to be chosen divided by the prior probability of the written value
(Corollary~\ref{cor:surprise}). Reading values therefore helps only against improbable values or a
concentrated choice of neuron.

\para{Relation to PCPs of proximity and holographic proofs.} The verifiers of PCPs of proximity and
of holographic proofs~\cite{bghsv2006robust,bfls1991} read only part of their input. Ours reads all
of its input, the trace. Honest recomputation moves the change from the trace into the weights, so
reading the whole trace does not help, and the model, not the trace, must be encoded.

\subsection{In-Range Edits on OPT-1.3B}
\label{ext:attack-inrange}
Per-neuron ranges are the minimum (0, after ReLU) and the maximum, or the 99.9th percentile, of each FFN
activation (the input of \texttt{fc2}) over 64 windows of 128 tokens of WikiText-2 train, every position. At
the last position of one layer, a greedy attacker moves the neurons with the largest first-order decrease of
the margin between the top-1 and the runner-up logit to the bound of their range, until the next token changes
or 256 neurons are edited (fp32, the 40 prompts of the OPT-6.7B experiment). Uniform detection per path is
$k/d_{\mathrm{ff}}$ ($d_{\mathrm{ff}}=8{,}192$); a contribution-weighted sampler opens neuron $j$ with
probability proportional to $|a_j|\,\|W_{\mathrm{fc2}}[:,j]\|$ on the claimed trace.

Pushing neurons to the top of their range raises their contribution, so contribution weighting catches those
edits often; lowering activations to zero, which stays in range after ReLU, hides them from it completely, at
the price of more neurons and fewer successes in early layers. Under uniform sampling every variant needs tens
of thousands of paths for $1-2^{-40}$ (19{,}000--227{,}000 at the medians).
