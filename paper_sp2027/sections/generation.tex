\section{Generation and Sampling}
\label{sec:gen}

\subsection{A Generated Response as One Prefill}
\label{sec:gen-prefill}

Proving each prefix of a response as its own query repeats the prompt: with $P=512$ prompt and
$G=512$ generated tokens, Llama-2-7B's mode-$\sC$ proofs take about \GenPerPrefixTB{}\,TB, against
\GenPrefillGB{}\,GB for one prefill.

\para{Reduction.} The response $y_1,\dots,y_G$ to a prompt $x$ is the greedy decoding if each
$y_i$ is the first largest logit at position $P+i-1$ of the model run on
$x\,\|\,y_1\cdots y_{i-1}$. Multipliers and tables are fixed at calibration and only causal
attention mixes positions, so a decoder with a KV cache computes exactly the integers of a causal
prefill of $s=x\,\|\,y_1\cdots y_{G-1}$. We prove one query on $s$, with $T=P+G-1$ positions and
the LM head at the last $G$ positions; their number enters the graph digest (Section~\ref{sec:fs}).

\para{Token rule.} First, the verifier requires that, for each $i<G$, column $i$ of the claimed
$V\times G$ logits have its first largest entry at the next input token $y_i$. The last column
gives $y_G$.

\para{Soundness.} The soundness error is the $\varepsilon$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} for this
graph, independent of $G$. A $y_i$ that is not the greedy token passes the token rule only if the
claimed logits at position $P+i-1$ are wrong, and the first-wrong-layer argument applies
unchanged. Claims and tokens are fixed before the challenges. In $\Kpre$, $Q$ in the reuse
bound counts the verdicts the prover sees.

\para{Cost.} Each matrix is opened and folded once per response, so the proof grows only by the
claims of the new positions. After a 512-token prompt (Fiat--Shamir, random int8 weights, full
models; the prover on one \PlatformGpu{}, the verifier on 8 threads of an \PlatformCpu{}), a greedy
256-token Llama-2-7B response takes \GenLlamaProveC{}\,s to prove, \GenLlamaVerifyC{}\,s to verify and
\GenLlamaProofC{} in mode $\sC$, and \GenLlamaProveK{}\,s, \GenLlamaVerifyK{}\,s and \GenLlamaProofK{} in $\Kpre$; Qwen3-4B takes
\GenQwenProveC{}\,s, \GenQwenVerifyC{}\,s and \GenQwenProofC{} in $\sC$. Llama-2-7B's proof per generated token falls from
\GenLlamaMBPerTokenShort{}\,MB at $G=64$ to \GenLlamaMBPerTokenLong{}\,MB at $G=256$, and a sampled response costs the
same as a greedy one within \GenSampledWithin{}\%. For GPT-2 after a 64-token prompt, proving costs
\GenGptMsPerToken{}\,ms per generated token; zkAgent reports 0.30\,s per token for GPT-2, on other hardware and
with other guarantees~\cite{zkagent}. Times exclude decoding, after which the prover computes the
trace of $s$. A tampered response was always rejected. Every cell is in the extended
version~\cite{fullversion}.

\subsection{Sampled Generation}
\label{sec:gen-sampling}

A token rule that accepted any plausible sampled token would let the provider choose the output.
Instead, the client sends a fresh 32-byte seed with each query, and the response must be the
output of a public, exact integer sampler keyed by that seed.

\para{The sampler.} It uses the Gumbel-max trick~\cite{gumbel1954,maddison2014astar}: sampling
from $\mathrm{softmax}(l/\tau)$ is choosing $\arg\max_v(l_v/\tau+g_v)$ with independent standard
Gumbel noise $g_v$. For claimed integer logits $z$ with real values $cz$, the temperature becomes
the integer weight $a=\mathrm{round}(2^{16}c/\tau)$, and the noise of entry $v$ is $\Gamma[u_v]$,
where $u_v$ is the $v$-th 16-bit word of
$\mathrm{SHAKE256}(\texttt{pvi/sampler/v1}\,\|\,\mathit{seed}\,\|\,\mathit{position})$ and
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma[i] = \mathrm{round}\Big(2^{16}\cdot\big({-\ln}\big({-\ln}\tfrac{i+1/2}{2^{16}}\big)\big)\Big),
  \qquad 0\le i<2^{16}.
  \label{eq:gumbel-table}
\end{equation}
The token is the first $v$ that maximises $az_v+\Gamma[u_v]$ among the $k$ largest entries of $z$
(all if $k=0$; ties go to the smaller index). Since $|z|<2^{29}$, $a<2^{24}$ and
$|\Gamma[i]|<2^{20}$, scores fit in 64-bit integers and every platform chooses the same token.
The token rule uses this choice in place of the argmax, and Fiat--Shamir absorbs $a$, $k$ and the
seed.

\para{Soundness and the seed.} The sampler adds no soundness term: the verifier recomputes each
token from the checked logits, so a wrong token requires a wrong logit. A provider that chose the
seed could try seeds until it obtained a preferred response; the client's seed, bound into the
statement, prevents this. Convincing a third party that the seed was random would need a jointly
generated seed (out of scope).

\para{Distribution.} The sampler approximates sampling at temperature $\tau$ with uniform
variables quantised to steps of $2^{-16}$. Since $\Gamma[i]/2^{16}$ lies in about
$[-2.47,11.78]$, a token whose temperature-scaled logit is more than about 14.25 below the largest
is never drawn; exact sampling draws it with probability below $e^{-14.25}<6.5\cdot10^{-7}$. Over
a vocabulary of $V$ tokens the excluded probability is thus below $(V-1)e^{-14.25}$: about 0.02 for Llama-2
(32{,}000 tokens), 0.03 for GPT-2 and OPT (about 50{,}000) and 0.1 for Qwen3 (151{,}936).
Computed exactly from the integer OPT-1.3B's logits at \TvPositions{} WikiText-2 positions, the sampler's
total-variation distance to $\mathrm{softmax}(cz/\tau)$ at $\tau=1$ has median \TvTopK{} with
$k=50$ and \TvFull{} with $k=0$, against \TvModel{} between the integer and the fp32 model's next-token
distributions. It comes from the rounding of $a$ and, with $k=0$, from the bounded upper tail of
$\Gamma$, which under-samples tokens 8 to 12 nats below a crowded top; a table of $2^{20}$ entries
would lower it to \TvTwenty{}. Nucleus (top-$p$) sampling is not implemented.
