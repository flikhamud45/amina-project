\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\para{Who the protocol serves.} It serves a CPU client of an open-weight model that holds neither
the weights nor a GPU and will not trust hardware, and a gateway or auditor with the GPU verifier. A
client with the weights and a GPU should re-execute. The regime is narrow
(Figure~\ref{fig:breakeven}; Llama-2-7B, Fiat--Shamir). Below about \BreakevenQueriesShort{} queries of 64 tokens,
mode-$\sC$ proofs cost the client fewer bytes than one download of the int8 weights; at 2{,}048
tokens the weights are only \BreakevenQueriesLong{}$\times$ the size of one proof, or \BreakevenQueriesVOne{}$\times$ with V1. A $\Kpre$ client that could keep the weights
verifies faster than exact re-execution on its CPU only on links faster than
\BreakevenLinkRange{}\,Gb/s\BreakevenVOneClause{}.

\para{Proof size.} The proof carries every weight operation's output, so it grows with the prompt.
For Llama-2-7B it reaches the size of the int8 weights at about \BreakevenTokens{} tokens without V1 and at about
\BreakevenTokensVOne{} with it. Committing activations, as zkLLM does, avoids this growth.

\para{Weights and trust.} The protocol is not zero-knowledge: the claims of about
$\lceil k/T\rceil$ queries reveal a weight matrix with rows of length $k$. It therefore targets
open-weight models. With private weights, the client would not learn which model the commitment
binds, as Hollow-LLM shows for zkSNARKs~\cite{hollowllm}.

\para{Scope.} We evaluate dense decoders, in full up to 2{,}048 tokens; mixture-of-experts models
are untested. Up to 16{,}384 tokens we extrapolate Llama-2-7B from 2- to 4-block builds with random weights of its shapes (within \LongCheckMax{}\% of the full model; real
OPT weights cost about as much, Section~\ref{sec:eval-setup}); there the proof reaches about
\LongProofLong{}\,GB. At 2{,}048 tokens the certified OPT models stay close to the floating-point
model, but our integer Qwen3-4B does not (\QwenLRatio{}$\times$); Section~\ref{sec:quality} also
reports Llama-2-7B (\LlamaSevenRatio{}$\times$).

\para{The attack and the bound.} Condition (H) is proved for random ensembles with independent rows
and for the constructed worst case, not for trained networks, where our evidence is the best
attacks found, including in-range edits of OPT-1.3B and OPT-6.7B (Section~\ref{sec:break}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Spot-checking an unencoded trace is not fully sound: an edit followed by honest recomputation is
the honest trace of a model that differs in one row, which in the worst case no sampler finds
faster than uniform sampling. Freivalds' checks of every weight operation against a linear-code
commitment restore full soundness with only a hash function and a prover orders of magnitude
faster than zkSNARK provers. This serves a client with neither the weights nor a GPU; the cost is a proof that grows with the prompt, which V1 shrinks.
