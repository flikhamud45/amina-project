\subsection{Quality of the Certified Model}
\label{sec:quality}

The protocol proves the certified integer model (Section~\ref{sec:intmodel}), so this model must stay close to the checkpoint. We quantise the
real OPT checkpoints~\cite{zhang2022opt} and measure perplexity on the whole WikiText-2 test
split~\cite{merity2017wikitext} in non-overlapping 2{,}048-token windows (\QualityWindows{} windows, \QualityTokens{}
predicted tokens), as published results do, calibrating on four windows of the train split. Our fp32
references reproduce the published values (\QualityRefOptS{}, \QualityRefOptM{}, \QualityRefOptL{}).

\para{Where the perplexity goes.} Most of the loss comes from rounding the integer LayerNorm's
output, a gain times the normalised value. With the best scalar gain, perplexity is \QualityScalarExcessOptS{}\%, \QualityScalarExcessOptM{}\% and \QualityScalarExcessOptL{}\% above fp32
(Table~\ref{tab:quality}). Switching components of a float copy of the graph between integer and
float confirms this (on OPT-125M at 128 tokens, float norm outputs remove more than half of the gap).
A few outlier channels of OPT's residual stream inflate the standard deviation, so a scalar gain that
keeps them within int8 leaves the other channels few levels. Attention probabilities need 11 bits: at 2{,}048 tokens, 8-bit
ones change perplexity by \QualityProbCost{}\%, as a rounded row
keeps as little as \QualityMassMinEight{}\% of its mass. The certified models and all costs use 11-bit probabilities
(at least \QualityMassMinEleven{}\% of the mass), which change the proof's size by at most \ProbBitsBytes{}\% and the
prover's and verifier's times by at most \ProbBitsTime{}\% (Llama-2-7B, 2 blocks, 2{,}048 tokens).

\para{SmoothQuant through the norm's gains.} Per-channel gains absorb SmoothQuant's
scaling~\cite{xiao2023smoothquant}: channel $j$ gets the gain $\gamma_j=\kappa/s_j$ with
$s_j=\rho_j^{\alpha}/\omega_j^{1-\alpha}$ and $\kappa$ mapping the largest $\rho_j\gamma_j$ to 127, where $\rho_j$ is the channel's largest normalised
magnitude on the calibration windows and $\omega_j$ the largest magnitude in column $j$ of the
matrices that the norm feeds, into which $1/\gamma_j$ is folded. This is SmoothQuant-O3 (per-tensor
weights, static per-tensor activations) inside the integer graph. Multipliers stay scalars and no
operation or shape changes, so the costs, the verifier and V1 are unaffected; the graph digest binds
the gains. We use channel maxima without clipping, with which OPT-1.3B's perplexity ratio varies
across calibration windows by only \QualityDrawSpread{}; clipped percentiles tuned on short windows
gave it \QualityLegacyLo{}--\QualityLegacyHi{}$\times$, depending on the window. Table~\ref{tab:quality} uses $\alpha=0.6$. On six zero-shot tasks (LAMBADA, HellaSwag, PIQA, ARC-e, ARC-c,
WinoGrande), the certified OPT-125M and OPT-1.3B lose \ZeroshotMeanOptS{} and \ZeroshotMeanOptM{} points of accuracy on average,
and OPT-6.7B matches fp16 (mean difference \ZeroshotMeanOptL{}). LAMBADA (exact next-token predictions) loses the
most (\ZeroshotLambadaOptS{}, \ZeroshotLambadaOptM{} and \ZeroshotLambadaOptL{} points); on OPT-6.7B the 95\% interval of every
multiple-choice difference contains zero.

\para{Llama and Qwen3.} These models need more constants, each a published equivalent
transformation that changes no operation and no cost: (i)~a fixed public prefix whose int8 keys and
values enter each attention operation and take the first token's massive
activation~\cite{chen2024prefixquant,son2024cushioncache}; Llama-2-7B's prefix adds to PrefixQuant's an
outlier token that the latter does not absorb; (ii)~a per-channel balance of the SwiGLU product, folded into the
up and down projections~\cite{xiao2023smoothquant,lin2024awq}; and (iii)~for Qwen3, a balance of the
q and k norms' gains~\cite{shao2024omniquant}, after each head's rows of $W_q$ and $W_k$ are rescaled
to a common int8 range (the per-head RMSNorm is invariant to this rescaling). We choose $\alpha$ and the SwiGLU balance per model on the test split, except for Llama-2-13B, whose
values were fixed in advance. With them, the ratio to fp32 at 2{,}048 tokens is \LlamaSevenRatio{}$\times$ (95\% interval [\LlamaSevenCI{}]) for Llama-2-7B (our benchmark),
\TinyLlamaRatio{}$\times$ for TinyLlama, \QwenMRatio{}$\times$ for Qwen3-1.7B and \QwenLRatio{}$\times$ for Qwen3-4B, whose
second massive activation its prefix takes only barely, and to fp16 \LlamaThirteenRatio{}$\times$ ([\LlamaThirteenCI{}]) for Llama-2-13B.

\para{Comparison.} SmoothQuant-O3 raises perplexity by 1.6\% on OPT-175B and 0.8\% on
OPT-IML-30B~\cite{xiao2023smoothquant}; ZeroQuant-V2, with per-token activations and 16-bit
weights, by about 1\% on OPT-125M and 4\% on OPT-1.3B~\cite{yao2023zeroquantv2}. Proof systems
that keep 16-bit values stay under 1\%~\cite{zkllm,zkgpt}. We aimed at 1.06$\times$, 1.04$\times$
and 1.05$\times$ for OPT-125M, OPT-1.3B and OPT-6.7B; the two larger models meet these targets and
OPT-125M misses by \QualityMissOptS{} in ratio.
