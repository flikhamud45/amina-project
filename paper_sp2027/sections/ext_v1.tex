\subsection{Details of the Windowed LogUp}

\para{Exceptions.} Clamps are rare: with real weights they occur at a rate of about \VOneRealOptLExceptionRate{} (the
2{,}048-token proof of the real OPT-6.7B lists \VOneRealOptLExceptions{}, \VOneRealOptLExceptionsMB{}\,MB), and with random weights our
measured proofs list at most \VOneRandomExceptionsMax{}. Residual operations clamp only after the addition
and have no exceptions.

\para{Field.} Under Fiat--Shamir, with the 64 extra bits of Section~\ref{sec:fs} in $\beta$, the
lookup term of Theorem~\ref{thm:v1}, about $(\Nleaf+|\Vtab|)/p^d$ over an extension of BabyBear of
degree $d$, stays below $2^{-\beta}$ on our largest queries only from $d=8$ (Table~\ref{tab:params}).
We use $\Fext =
\F[\xi]/(\xi^8 - 11)$, a field because 11 is a quadratic non-residue modulo $p$ and $p \equiv 1
\pmod 4$, and we use it in interactive runs as well.

\para{Table.} One table serves every width with one leaf per entry. It has at most
$\ParamsTable{}$ elements for the full Llama-2-7B with random weights (Table~\ref{tab:params}) and
$\VOneRealOptLTable{}$ for the real OPT-6.7B.

\para{Both integer conditions are needed.} Lemma~\ref{lem:v1-logup} needs both of its integer
conditions. If a multiplicity may reach $p$, a prover holding $p$ copies of an off-table leaf adds
$p$ to the multiplicity of any table element, which keeps the sum and the identity. Without the sum
check it simply leaves those leaves out. Our tests confirm the lemma and both counterexamples by
exhaustive search on $\mathbb F_{49} = \mathbb F_7[\xi]/(\xi^2 - 3)$.

\para{Relation to deployed logUp.} The logUp lemma assumes a characteristic larger than the number
of looked-up values~\cite[Lemma~5]{haboeck2022logup}, and the systems over 31-bit fields that we
checked keep that number below $p$. S-two, over $\mathbb F_{2^{31}-1}$, lets its verifier check a
bound on the number of lookups derived from the table heights~\cite[Remark~17]{stwo2026};
OpenVM, over BabyBear, caps the number of interactions at $p$ and treats multiplicities as integers
when one side sums to less than $p$~\cite{openvm2025logup}; and SP1 includes the clock in every
query, so that repeated queries differ~\cite{lurk2024lookups}. A V1 query has up to
$\VOneLargeLeaves{}>p$ leaves (Llama-2-13B at 2{,}048 tokens), which such a bound would force us to split into instances with separate
multiplicity vectors and separate checks. Lemma~\ref{lem:v1-logup} uses instead that V1 sends its
multiplicities in the clear: the verifier checks $\mu_\tau<p$ and $\sum_\tau\mu_\tau=\Nleaf$ over
the integers, at a cost of $|\Vtab|$ additions, and the number of leaves is not bounded. A system
that commits to its multiplicities would need a range proof and an integer-sum proof for the
committed column instead.

\para{The tree and its transcript.} The cut operations are packed in graph order into instances of
at most $2^{n_{\max}}$ leaves. The leaves are $(1, \alpha - v)$ at real positions and $(0, 1)$ at
padding, and a binary tree adds sibling fractions, $(\mathsf n, \mathsf d) \oplus (\mathsf n',
\mathsf d') = (\mathsf n \mathsf d' + \mathsf n' \mathsf d,\, \mathsf d \mathsf d')$, so that the
root satisfies $\mathsf n_0 / \mathsf d_0 = \sum 1/(\alpha - v)$. An instance with $n$ variables
sends $4 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (3k + 4)$ elements of $\Fext$ of 31 bytes each, \GkrTranscriptKB{}\,kB for $n = \GkrTranscriptBits{}$.

\para{Transposed matrices.} For a transposed matrix the prover sends $y = A \bar x$, and the column
check combines the opened columns with the coordinates of $\bar x$.

\para{What V1 does not need.} V1 needs no new commitment, no NTT, no Merkle tree over activations
and no proximity test.

\para{Concrete security.} At $\lambda = 128$ the two new terms of Theorem~\ref{thm:v1} take two
extra shares of the union bound, which changes neither $r$ nor the column counts.
Table~\ref{tab:params} gives them for Llama-2-7B at 2{,}048 tokens, and the last subsection of this
part for Llama-2-13B, the largest model we consider.

\subsection{Implementation of V1}

V1 is implemented in Python and PyTorch for modes $\sC$ and $\Kpre$, interactive and under
Fiat--Shamir. The verifier checks an instance's round chains at once and
computes $L_\ell e$, $W_\ell e$ and $\bar x_\ell$ exactly from per-window tables (each window is
affine plus a step), with native int64 kernels on a CPU. The GKR
prover is written in Triton~\cite{tillet2019triton}: BabyBear elements in Montgomery
form~\cite{montgomery1985} on 32-bit lanes, an unrolled product in $\Fext$, and kernels for the
leaves, the tree, the eq tables, the round sums and the folds, with the host drawing each
challenge. Its transcripts are byte-identical to those of a reference prover in PyTorch (12 tests
on the GPU). Factoring the current variable's eq term out of each round polynomial saves 4 of the
20 products in $\Fext$ per pair. In the bottom layer, when rows are full ($T =
2^{n_{\mathrm{col}}}$), the first $n_{\mathrm{col}} - 1$ rounds bind position bits within rows whose
numerators are all 1; a dedicated path reads only the real rows' denominators, evaluates $\mathsf
d_L + \mathsf d_R + \eta\, \mathsf d_L \mathsf d_R$ per pair, and adds the padding rows' term $\eta
(1 - \sum \Veq)$ in closed form. On the \PlatformGpu{} an instance of $2^{25}$ leaves then takes
\GkrSecondsLarge{}\,s (\GkrNsPerLeaf{}\,ns per leaf). The test suite with V1 (1{,}492 tests)
passes, including honest queries in eight configurations, a tampered trace value with honestly
recomputed later layers (rejected at the final check in $\sC$ and at the secret-vector check in
$\Kpre$), fake or omitted exceptions, multiplicity cheats and tampered GKR and fold messages. V1
also accepts the sampled generation of Section~\ref{sec:gen}, since the LM head stays clear.

\subsection{Hardening of the V1 Checks}

An earlier version of the implementation lacked five of the properties stated in
Section~\ref{sec:v1} and in the checks of the soundness proof. None of the gaps allowed a forgery
against the models we evaluate. Two allowed forgeries on other models: the $\Kpre$ verifier never
evaluated (P4), as its public graph has no weights (it now evaluates it from the weights when it derives its key), and a clamp wider
than int8 broke the bound on $|L|$ (now excluded by (P1)). Two concerned hygiene: folds were
accepted with coefficients in $[p, 2^{31})$ (an alias, not a forgery), and a defence-in-depth range
check on the inputs of cut operations was missing; both are now enforced. The fifth limits
completeness: more than $2^{29}$ clamped entries cannot be encoded, as the exception bin is outside
(P5). In mode $\sC$, (P4) is a property of the committed model, checked when the honest commitment
is computed; with an untrusted commitment (Section~\ref{sec:setup-proof}) the bound must be
published with the model, for v0 and V1 alike.

\para{Proof size.} At 2{,}048 tokens V1 is \VOneRatioRandomExact{}$\times$ smaller on every full
model; the full OPT-1.3B proof drops from \VOneOptMCVZeroGB{} to \VOneOptMCVOneGB{}\,GB in mode $\sC$ and from \VOneOptMKVZeroGB{} to
\VOneOptMKVOneGB{}\,GB in $\Kpre$. The values shrink \VOneValuesRatio{}$\times$: an int8 value, and a residual increment,
compress to fewer bits than a claim. The parts that do not grow with the
prompt grow instead: eight folded rows instead of $r$, $y$ over $\Fext$ for transposed matrices,
and the new multiplicities and GKR transcripts (at most \VOneExtrasMax{}\,MB in every measured build). GPT-2 at 64
tokens thus gains only \VOneRatioShortC{}$\times$ ($\sC$) and \VOneRatioShortK{}$\times$ ($\Kpre$), and the Llama-2-13B build of
Table~\ref{tab:eval-v1}, whose opened columns are a larger share of its proof, \VOneRatioLargeC{}$\times$ in $\sC$.
V1's sizes come from an exact byte accounting that equals real queries to the byte except for the
Merkle multiproofs, whose size depends on the drawn columns; the timed full V1 queries agree with
it within \VOneAccountingWithin{}\%. The full Llama-2-7B at 2{,}048 tokens in $\sC$ gains \VOneLlamaCRatio{}$\times$
(\VOneLlamaCVZeroGB{} to \VOneLlamaCVOneGB{}\,GB). With real weights, the real OPT-6.7B measures \VOneRatioRealOptLExact{}$\times$ in $\Kpre$
at 2{,}048 tokens (\VOneRealOptLVZeroGB{} to \VOneRealOptLVOneGB{}\,GB, with \VOneRealOptLExceptions{} exceptions). On the certified OPT-1.3B the
gain is \VOneRealOptMLongC{}$\times$ in $\sC$ and \VOneRealOptMLongK{}$\times$ in $\Kpre$ at 2{,}048 tokens (random weights:
\VOneRandomOptMLongC{}$\times$ and \VOneRandomOptMLongK{}$\times$) and \VOneRealOptMShortC{}$\times$ and \VOneRealOptMShortK{}$\times$ at 64 tokens
(random weights: \VOneRandomOptMShortC{}$\times$ and \VOneRandomOptMShortK{}$\times$), because real weights shrink v0's claims more than
V1's values; real OPT weights also have residual multipliers
above $2^{29}$, whose operations stay clear (P3).

\para{Prover and verifier.} With the GKR on the GPU and instances of at most $2^{\ParamsLmax{}}$ leaves, the
prover pays about \VOneNsPerClaim{}\,ns per cut claim end to end, \VOneProverShare{}\% of its time. At 2{,}048 tokens it takes
\VOneProverLong{}\,s, \VOneProverRatioLong{}$\times$ the time of v0's prover, whose int8 forward pass is fast on this
GPU. The CPU verifier takes \VOneVerifyRatioLong{}$\times$ v0's verifier's time at 2{,}048 tokens and
\VOneVerifyRatioMedium{}$\times$ for GPT-2 at 512 tokens, mostly for the final check's two exact products per claim,
$L_\ell e$ and $W_\ell e$. On the full Llama-2-7B in $\Kpre$ at 2{,}048 tokens (8 threads of the
\PlatformCpu{}, Fiat--Shamir), V1's verifier takes \VOneVerifyLlama{}\,s with the batched GKR check and a native kernel for
the final check's products, against \VOneVerifyLlamaBaseline{}\,s with the verifier they replace, on the same
queries, and v0's verifier \VOneVZeroVerifyLlama{}\,s. V1 thus trades prover time
for a proof \VOneRatioRandom{}$\times$ smaller with random weights, which pays off where moving and storing
bytes costs more than seconds of GPU time.

\subsection{Limitations of V1}

Mode $\sK$ and the streaming GPU verifier do not support V1 yet \todo{GPU verifier with V1}. V1
assumes one requantisation multiplier per operation, so per-row weight scales
(Section~\ref{sec:quality}) need more widths, and CNNs that fuse ReLU into the requantisation are
not covered. By the byte model the full Llama-2-7B proof still reaches the size of its int8 weights
at about \BreakevenTokensVOne{} tokens instead of about \BreakevenTokens{}, and the sent values, like the claims, reveal
information about the weights.

\subsection{GKR Instances for the Largest Model}

For Llama-2-13B at 2{,}048 tokens and $\lambda = 128$, the cut operations of mode $\sC$ form
\VOneLargeInstances{} GKR instances of at most $2^{\VOneLargeBits{}}$ leaves each, with $\Nleaf=\VOneLargeLeaves{}$. Their
terms in Theorem~\ref{thm:v1} sum to $2^{\VOneLargeGkrLog{}}$, against at most $2^{\VOneLargeLookupLog{}}$ for the lookup term.
