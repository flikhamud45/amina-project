\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

\para{Implementation.} The protocol is written in Python and PyTorch, with Triton kernels for the GPU
verifier and C++/OpenMP kernels for the CPU verifier. Our \emph{streaming} GPU verifier uploads the
claims one weight operation at a time, overlapping the upload with its checks. Unless stated otherwise,
$\lambda=128$, every cell uses Fiat--Shamir, and mode~$\sC$ uses codewords of at least $2^{\PlanLogPoints{}}$ points.

\para{Platform.} All our measurements come from one run of the artifact: one \PlatformGpu{} runs the
prover, the GPU verifier and GPU re-execution, and 8 threads of an \PlatformCpu{} the CPU verifier
and CPU re-execution. Other systems' costs are quoted from their papers; we also ran zkLLM's public
code on the \PlatformGpu{} \ZkllmSameGpuRun{}. Times are medians after a warm-up (\OptionsRuns{} runs
in Table~\ref{tab:options}, min--max spread $\le$\OptionsSpread{}\%).

\para{Models.} The image models are the six of Section~\ref{sec:break}. The language models are
GPT-2, OPT-125M/1.3B/6.7B, Llama-2-7B/13B and Qwen3-4B, built from their checkpoints with the
integer recipes of Section~\ref{sec:quality}; GPT-2, and prompts beyond a checkpoint's trained
context (4{,}096 tokens and more), use random int8 weights of the same shapes. Weight values matter
little: on the certified OPT-1.3B (both modes, 64 and 2{,}048 tokens) the prover's and the CPU
verifier's times are within \RealProveWithin{}\% and \RealVerifyWithin{}\% of those with random
weights, and v0's proof is \RealProofSmaller{}\% smaller with the real ones. In $\Kpre$ at 2{,}048 tokens,
\RealAcceptedClause{}, and every tampered proof is rejected. The last block computes only the final
position, the one the logits need. Models are measured in full unless a table marks a build of $b$
of their $L$ blocks as $b/L$.

\para{Soundness tests.} Section~\ref{sec:break} reports the attack measurements. Our verifier
accepted all \FinalNHonest{} honest queries and rejected all \FinalNAttacks{} attacks (one changed
value or neuron, perturbed weights, substitute models, forgeries that pass all checks but one):
Freivalds' check rejected \FinalNFreivalds{}, the column check \FinalNColumnsCode{}, the Merkle
check \FinalNColumnsMerkle{} and the range check \FinalNRange{}\TodoCounts{}.

\para{Costs.} In mode~$\sC$, the image models are proved in \CnnProveRange{}\,ms and verified in
\CnnVerifyRange{}\,ms, with proofs \CnnProofRatio{}$\times$ smaller than their int8 weights.
Llama-2-7B's 64-token proof is about \ProofRatioLlamaShort{}$\times$ smaller than its int8 weights
(Table~\ref{tab:eval-llm}). At 2{,}048 tokens the proof and the CPU verifier's time grow (OPT-6.7B:
\ProofOptLLong{}, \VerifyOptLLong{}): the claims, which grow with the prompt, make up
\ClaimsShareLlama{}\% of Llama-2-7B's \ProofLlamaLongCExact{} proof, and the verifier recomputes
attention over all position pairs. There the GPU verifier is \GpuVerifyFactor{}$\times$ faster than
the CPU one; a CPU client of Llama-2-7B in $\Kpre$ needs \ClientPeakLongKpre{}\,GB of memory. On the
\PlatformGpu{}, proving a 2{,}048-token OPT-1.3B prompt in $\Kpre$ and $\sC$ costs
\ServeOptMKpreOverUOne{} and \ServeOptMCOverUOne{}$\times$ our exact integer forward pass without
proofs, and \ServeOptMKpreOverUTwo{} and \ServeOptMCOverUTwo{}$\times$ an fp16 one. Proving 64
of its 64-token prompts together in $\Kpre$ takes \ServeBatchKpre{}\,s, \ServeBatchOverUOne{} and
\ServeBatchOverUTwo{}$\times$ the two forward passes\TodoServing{}.

\para{Implementation improvements.} None changes a byte that the parties exchange or a verdict; the
extended version~\cite{fullversion} details each and measures it against the path it replaces. The prover
splits field elements into signed bytes so that the column opening and the fold are exact int8 GEMMs
(\ImproveOpenRange{}$\times$ faster on Llama-2 blocks), runs the weight products on int8 tensor cores and
attention in a fused exact kernel without a $T\times T$ buffer, frees activations once used, and writes the
encoded claims on several threads (\ImproveEncodeRange{}$\times$). The GPU verifier decodes the claims on the
GPU and reuses the fused attention (\ImproveGpuvRange{}$\times$ at 2{,}048 tokens). The CPU verifier uses
native kernels for every phase, int32 claims and a tuned allocator, and derives the $\Kpre$ key with native
field products. V1's verifier batches its final checks and its prover runs the GKR rounds in Triton. To fit
the GPU's memory, the Llama-2 GPU-verifier cells decode the claims one stream at a time and V1's Llama-2-7B
cells keep the GKR's layers in bounded memory; neither mode changes a byte.

Table~\ref{tab:options} sets our verifier against exact re-execution, which only a client holding
the weights can run, and against the other options of a client.

\para{CPU.} With native exact kernels for every phase (among them an AVX-512 integer attention), our
$\Kpre$ verifier checks a 2{,}048-token query of the full Llama-2-7B in \CpuVerifyLong{}\,s on 8
threads of the \PlatformCpu{}. Exact re-execution on the same threads (the integer forward pass with
\CpuReexecName{}) takes \CpuReexecLong{}\,s and needs the \WeightsLlama{} of weights, where the
verifier holds a \KpreStoreGB{}\,GB key. At 64 tokens the two take \CpuVerifyShort{}\,s and
\CpuReexecShort{}\,s. Inexact inference of the same weights (PyTorch, llama.cpp~\cite{llamacpp}),
which cannot confirm the claims bit for bit, takes at least \CpuInexactLong{}\,s at 2{,}048 tokens
(\CpuInexactName{}).

\para{Break-even.} A client that receives a proof of $B$ bytes over a link of $R$ bit/s and
verifies it in time $t_v$ beats a re-execution taking $t_r$ when $8B/R+t_v<t_r$.
Figure~\ref{fig:breakeven} plots the bytes of $Q$ mode-$\sC$ queries of 64 or 2{,}048 tokens (also
with V1) against one download of the \WeightsLlamaGB{}\,GB of int8 weights, and this condition for a
$\Kpre$ client's 2{,}048-token query against exact re-execution on the same CPU threads.

\para{GPU.} On a GPU, re-execution is faster: on the \PlatformGpu{}, our streaming GPU verifier
checks a 2{,}048-token OPT-1.3B query in $\Kpre$ in \OptMGpuVerify{}\,s, against \OptMGpuFloat{}\,s
for fp16 re-execution on the same GPU\TodoGpuOneGpu{}. The GPU verifier suits an auditor or gateway
that checks a provider without holding the weights.

Table~\ref{tab:eval-compare} sets our costs against published
systems~\cite{zkcnn,zkml,zkgpt,deepprove,zktorch,maverick,zkllm} on matched models. The guarantees
differ: the zkSNARKs also prove the operations without weights, and many hide the weights, whereas
our verifier recomputes those operations and our protocol is not zero-knowledge. The hardware
differs too: our prover runs on a GPU, and all the zkSNARKs but zkLLM ran on CPUs. On the same
\PlatformGpu{}, zkLLM's prover takes about \ZkllmSameGpuProverLlama{}\,s for a 2{,}048-token
Llama-2-7B prompt and \ZkllmSameGpuProverLlamaL{}\,s for Llama-2-13B, \ZkllmSameGpuFactorRange{}$\times$
ours, and \ZkllmSameGpuFactorShortRange{}$\times$ at \ZkllmSameGpuShortSeq{} tokens. Overall, our prover is faster in every row, by
\CompareProverMin{}$\times$ to about \CompareProverMax{}$\times$ against the zkSNARKs and \CompareMaverick{}$\times$ against Maverick; our
verifier is slower in \CompareVerifierSlowerRows{}; and our proof is larger in most rows, by up to
\CompareProofMax{}$\times$ against zkLLM, whose proof stays below 200\,kB. V1 narrows that gap on
OPT-1.3B from about \CompareVOneBefore{}$\times$ to about \CompareVOneAfter{}$\times$. We do not
compare sampling-based schemes such as CommitLLM (Table~\ref{tab:sok}), which skip operations.
