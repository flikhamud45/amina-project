\section{Attacking Spot-Checking Proofs of Inference}
\label{sec:attack}

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Setting and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

The model $M$ has a public architecture. The client (the verifier) may not hold its weights and
obtains a commitment $C_M$ to them before any query. For each query $x$ the provider (the
prover) returns an output $y$ and a proof. We require \emph{completeness} and \emph{full soundness}
with error $\varepsilon$: any $y\neq M(x)$ is accepted with probability at most $\varepsilon$.
Anchuri et al.~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable} prove only \emph{other-model soundness}, against a
provider that honestly runs a different model.

\para{Spot-checking.} The provider commits to the execution trace, and the verifier checks a few
\emph{sites} against committed data: neurons with their incoming weights in the path protocol,
chunks of layers in SLP~\cite{slp}, (layer, token) cells in CommitLLM's routine
audit~\cite{commitllm2026}. The trace has no error-correcting encoding, so a changed value is
visible at one site only.

\para{Adversary.} The provider may deviate arbitrarily but keeps the committed weights. It knows
the sampling rule, even one that adapts to the claimed values, but not the verifier's coins, and
chooses which queries to tamper with. As in~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}, $C_M$ is computed
honestly; for our defence, anyone can recompute it from the public fp16 checkpoint with a
deterministic, public quantisation recipe and fixed calibration data (Section~\ref{sec:setup-proof}
treats the other case).

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Path Protocol and the Single-Site Edit}
\label{sec:path}

In the path protocol of Anchuri et al.\ (RandPathTest), the prover Merkle-commits to every
activation~\cite{merkle1987}. The verifier walks from a random output neuron to the input through
random parents. At each node $j$ it opens the parents' activations $a_i$ and the weights $w_{ij}$,
and checks $a_j=\phi\big(\sum_i w_{ij}a_i\big)$ up to a floating-point tolerance. Anchuri et al.\
note that a path catches one changed node with probability at most $1/N$ in a layer of width $N$,
and suggest more paths, or sampling where it matters.

\para{The single-site edit.} Our adversary runs the correct model on $x$ and changes the
activation of one neuron $u$ so that the output becomes wrong, or a class of its choice. It then
recomputes all later layers \emph{with the true weights}, so only $u$ fails its check, and $s$
paths miss it with probability $(1-1/N)^s$. Applied only to triggered inputs, the edit is a
backdoor~\cite{gu2019badnets} in the serving code: the weights stay clean, and clean queries are
answered bit for bit as before. Repeated queries do not help: the provider tampers only with the
$k$ queries it cares about, and is caught with probability at most $1-(1-1/N)^k\le k/N$.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Why Sampling Cannot Fix It}
\label{sec:sampling}

\begin{definition}[spot-checking verifiers]
\label{def:spot}
Fix a \emph{target layer} of width $N$. Row $\theta_i$ holds the incoming weights and bias of
neuron $i$, whose value on $x$ is $h_i=f_i(\theta_i)$. The verifier knows all other layers and the
\emph{view} $P=(x,Z,\tau')$: the query, public side information $Z$ (such as natural ranges) and
the whole claimed trace $\tau'$. It learns $\theta$ only through a commitment oracle, whose query
$\sigma$ returns a symbol that depends only on the rows in its \emph{support}
$S_\sigma\subseteq[N]$; the oracle has \emph{locality} $d$ if always $|S_\sigma|\le d$. A prover
message checked against the commitment, such as a folded row $\chi^{\top}\!A$, is a query whose
support is the set of rows on which the honest message depends. $V\in\mathrm{Spot}_d(q)$ chooses
each query from $P$, its coins and the earlier answers; always accepts the honest trace and oracle
of every \emph{admissible} instance (one consistent with $Z$); and makes in expectation at most $q$
queries that meet the target layer. It \emph{touches} neuron $u$ if some query has $u\in S_\sigma$.
\end{definition}

\noindent Row-wise openings have $d=1$ if they hide unopened rows (Remark~\ref{rem:merkle}).
Folded rows, the code columns of Section~\ref{sec:defence} and succinct arguments over the layer
have support $[N]$, so SNARK-assisted verifiers fall outside every class with $d<N$. We measure $q$
under the reference oracle $R_p$ of Definition~\ref{def:H}; it matches the real run until the
edited neuron is touched, all that the proof uses.

\para{Honest recomputation hides the edit.} The forged trace is the \emph{honest} trace of an
admissible model that differs in row $u$ alone (for a ReLU neuron: zero weights and bias $v$, or
$-1$ if $v=0$); such a forgery is \emph{explainable}. Without recomputation, the inconsistent
children of $u$ would betray it.

\begin{theorem}[spot-checking is guessing the location]
\label{thm:guess}
Let $J$ be the neuron that the adversary edits, and suppose that the rows other than $J$ carry no
information about $J$ beyond the view (condition~(H) of Definition~\ref{def:H}; it holds, for
example, when the rows are independent and the adversary chooses $J$ from the values $h$ alone).
Then every $V\in\mathrm{Spot}_d(q)$ rejects an explainable single-site forgery with probability at
most
\[
  G_{qd}(J\mid P) \;=\; \mathbb E_{p}\Big[\max_{|S|\le qd}\Pr[J\in S\mid P=p]\Big],
\]
the probability of guessing $J$ in $qd$ tries from the view alone. For $d=1$ the bound is attained
by a verifier that knows the adversary's strategy and opens the $q$ neurons most likely to have
been edited.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Until a query touches $J$, the answers equal those under the explanation, so $V$ runs as on an
honest admissible instance and accepts. By~(H), the probability $x_u$ that $V$ touches $u$ does
not depend on whether $J=u$, and $\sum_u x_u\le qd$. Detection is thus a fractional knapsack worth
at most $G_{qd}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:attack}).
\end{proof}

\noindent Opening rows only confirms that they are clean, as in the search for a hidden
object~\cite{chew1967,kadane1971}. Condition~(H) is needed: a row that stores the location of the
critical neuron lets two adaptive queries catch the edit (Remark~\ref{rem:pointer}). Trained
networks have correlated rows; for them we rely on the worst case of Section~\ref{sec:dichotomy}.
With independent rows, the posterior on the edited neuron is proportional to its propensity to be
chosen divided by the prior probability of the written value (Corollary~\ref{cor:surprise}), so
reading values helps only against improbable values or a concentrated choice of neuron.

\subsection{A Lower Bound and a Matching Upper Bound}
\label{sec:dichotomy}

On a suitable network (H) holds by construction. This yields a lower bound for every
spot-checking verifier, matched at full locality by our defence.

\begin{theorem}[lower and upper bound]
\label{thm:dich}
Consider verifiers that read each layer's weights through a commitment of locality $d$ that
reveals nothing about unopened rows, and let $q$ be the expected number of queries that meet a
layer of width $N$.
(a)~\emph{Spot-checking.} For every such verifier there is a ReLU network with a hidden layer of
width $N$, and a single-site forgery with every value in its natural range, that the verifier
accepts with probability at least $1-qd/N$, even if it knows the later weights, every neuron's
natural range and the natural distribution of each neuron of that layer. In particular, a verifier
that spot-checks $q$ of the $N$ sites of an unencoded trace ($d=1$) accepts with probability at
least $1-q/N$, whatever its sampler, and soundness error at most $\varepsilon$ on every such
network requires $qd\ge(1-\varepsilon)N$.
(b)~\emph{Mixing.} If each query mixes all rows ($d=N$), as the folded rows and code columns of
Section~\ref{sec:defence} do, then under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:amp},
$O(\lambda+\log L)$ queries per layer give soundness error $2^{-\lambda}$ for a network of $L$
weight layers. For $L\le2^{\lambda}$ this is $O(\lambda)$, so $qd=O(\lambda)N$ suffices.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
For~(a), hidden neuron $i$ copies input bit $\sigma(i)$ for a uniform permutation $\sigma$, and
the output is the AND of the hidden neurons. On a natural input with its one zero at $\zeta$,
setting the neuron that copies $\zeta$ to $1$ flips the output within the range $\{0,1\}$. Nothing in
the view depends on $\sigma$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:guess} bounds detection by $qd/N$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:worst}). Part~(b) is our defence's soundness argument for one layer
(Proposition~\ref{prop:amp}).
\end{proof}

\para{What the bounds mean.} In the worst case nothing beats uniform sampling, which reaches $s/N$
on every dense network; $2^{-40}$ then takes about $27.7N$ paths per query. Tightness at
intermediate locality $1<d<N$ is open, and we do not claim that the instance of~(a) embeds in a
transformer (Remark~\ref{rem:scope}). The lower bound formalises a folklore calculation
(Section~\ref{sec:related}) for adaptive, value-aware verifiers. Unlike the
verifiers of PCPs of proximity and holographic proofs~\cite{bghsv2006robust,bfls1991}, ours reads
all of its input, the trace; honest recomputation moves the change into the weights, so the model,
not the trace, must be encoded.

\subsection{Measured Attacks}
\label{sec:break}

We attacked our implementation of the path protocol on an MNIST MLP (784--512--256--10), LeNet-5,
VGG-11, VGG-16 and ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, a 224$\times$224 dog-vs-cat ResNet-18 that stands in for
the unreleased model of~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}, and the real OPT-6.7B, computing path
acceptance exactly. The implementation reproduces the published behaviour~\cite{fullversion}.

\para{MNIST.} The substitute models of Anchuri et al.\ corrupt hundreds of the MLP's 778 trace
nodes and are always caught; our forgeries leave 1--14 inconsistent nodes. One tampered neuron
passes with the predicted probability $1-1/N$ (99.6--99.8\%), and 250 paths 61\% of the time. A
backdoor triggered by a $3{\times}3$ patch leaves clean queries unchanged, succeeds on every
triggered query not already in the target class, and passed 99.96\% of 2{,}300 end-to-end runs
(predicted: 99.8\%). Keeping each tampered value below its neuron's 95th percentile takes about
five neurons and passes 99\% of the time under uniform sampling.\footnote{Percentiles computed on
test queries that include the attacked ones. \todo{G3: training-split ranges}}

\para{Other samplers.} Uniform sampling detects 0.20\% of attacks. The contribution-weighted
sampler, which steps to parent $i$ with probability $\propto|w_{ij}a_i|$, detects 9.2\% of
the best single-neuron edits but never visits a neuron whose claimed activation is zero:
zeroing about 14 of the 512 neurons flips the prediction and passed 2{,}000 of 2{,}000 runs. A
floor $\delta$, sampling by $|w_{ij}|(|a_i|+\delta)$, reaches 2\% at $\delta=1$. Each figure is the
detection of the best attack we found against that sampler, an upper bound on its worst case;
2\% is thus the best worst case among our samplers. \todo{G3: adaptive game, held-out ranges}

\para{Larger models.} On every CNN and test query, one changed penultimate neuron flips the
prediction and is caught by a path with probability 1/84 (LeNet-5) to 1/512 (VGG, ResNet), also on
the binary dog-vs-cat ResNet-18, for which Anchuri et al.\ state full soundness. $2^{-40}$ takes
2{,}316--14{,}182 paths, which open the whole model and trace, and at least 305{,}193 in the FFN
layers of Llama-2-7B (width 11{,}008), about 13.7\,GB of fp16 openings at 64 tokens.

\para{A real LLM.} On OPT-6.7B (fp16), one FFN neuron of layer 8, 16, 24 or 31 changed the next
token for all 40 prompts. A fixed-target backdoor, constrained by the final LayerNorm, succeeds
for ``\texttt{back}'' (40/40, clean prompts unchanged) and fails for ``\texttt{hacked}'' (0/40).
Its values exceed the natural ranges, which the path protocol does not check, and $2^{-40}$ needs
at least 454{,}248 paths. \todo{L40S: in-range attack (G3.5)} The extended
version~\cite{fullversion} gives the paths for $k$ edited neurons of two instruction-tuned models.

A single-site edit is caught exactly when the sample covers it (Table~\ref{tab:sok}). SLP,
CommitLLM, NanoZK and SPEX state probabilistic guarantees, and SLP and CommitLLM describe the
edit-then-recompute strategy themselves~\cite{slp,commitllm2026}. We do not call them broken, and
no other value-blind sample of the same size does better against every edit placement
(Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}). The path protocol's full soundness for binary
classifiers fails on our dog-vs-cat ResNet-18. TensorCommitments' stated goal of negligible
soundness is not achieved by its layer selection, which the provider can compute. Selection by
value is evaded by edits that write common values (our zeroing attack; blindfolded
updates~\cite{fang2023pol}). TOPLOC and SVIP claim only to detect substitution.
