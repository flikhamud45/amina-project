\section{Attacking Spot-Checking Proofs of Inference}
\label{sec:attack}

\subsection{Setting and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

The model $M$ has a public architecture. The client (the verifier) may not hold its weights and
obtains a commitment $C_M$ to them before any query. For each query $x$ the provider (the
prover) returns an output $y$ and a proof. We require \emph{completeness} and \emph{full soundness}
with error $\varepsilon$: any $y\neq M(x)$ is accepted with probability at most $\varepsilon$.
Anchuri et al.~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable} prove only \emph{other-model soundness}, against an
honest run of a different model.

\para{Spot-checking.} The provider commits to the execution trace, and the verifier checks a few
\emph{sites} against committed data: neurons with their incoming weights in the path protocol,
layer chunks in SLP~\cite{slp}, (layer, token) cells in CommitLLM~\cite{commitllm2026}. The trace
has no error-correcting encoding, so a changed value is visible at one site only.

\para{Adversary.} The provider may deviate arbitrarily but keeps the committed weights. It knows
the sampling rule, even one that adapts to the claimed values, but not the verifier's coins, and
chooses which queries to tamper with. As in~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}, $C_M$ is computed
honestly; for our defence, anyone can recompute it from the public fp16 checkpoint with a
deterministic, public quantisation recipe and fixed calibration data (Section~\ref{sec:setup-proof}
treats the other case). The statement binds token ids: the client tokenises its text with the model's
public tokenizer, and a system prompt that the provider prepends is part of $x$, which the client
receives and the proof covers; a hidden system prompt is beyond what any verifier can certify.

\subsection{The Path Protocol and the Single-Site Edit}
\label{sec:path}

In the path protocol of Anchuri et al.\ (RandPathTest), the prover Merkle-commits to every
activation~\cite{merkle1987}. The verifier walks from a random output neuron to the input through
random parents. At each node $j$ it opens the parents' activations $a_i$ and the weights $w_{ij}$,
and checks $a_j=\phi\big(\sum_i w_{ij}a_i\big)$ up to a floating-point tolerance. Against the
$1/N$ chance that a path catches one changed node in a layer of width $N$, Anchuri et al.\ suggest
more paths, or sampling where it matters.

\para{The single-site edit.} Our adversary runs the correct model on $x$ and changes the
activation of one neuron $u$ to make the output wrong, or a class of its choice. It then
recomputes all later layers \emph{with the true weights}, so only $u$ fails its check, and $s$
paths miss it with probability $(1-1/N)^s$. Applied only to triggered inputs, the edit is a
backdoor~\cite{gu2019badnets} in the serving code that leaves the weights clean and clean queries
bit for bit unchanged. Repeated queries do not help: a provider that tampers with $k$ queries is
caught with probability at most $1-(1-1/N)^k\le k/N$.

\subsection{Why Sampling Cannot Fix It}
\label{sec:sampling}

\begin{definition}[spot-checking verifiers]
\label{def:spot}
Fix a \emph{target layer} of width $N$. Row $\theta_i$ holds the incoming weights and bias of
neuron $i$, whose value on $x$ is $h_i=f_i(\theta_i)$. The verifier knows all other layers and the
\emph{view} $P=(x,Z,\tau')$: the query, public side information $Z$ (such as natural ranges) and
the whole claimed trace $\tau'$. It learns $\theta$ only through a commitment oracle, whose query
$\sigma$ returns a symbol that depends only on the rows in its \emph{support}
$S_\sigma\subseteq[N]$; the oracle has \emph{locality} $d$ if always $|S_\sigma|\le d$. A prover
message checked against the commitment, such as a folded row $\chi^{\top}\!A$, is a query whose
support is the set of rows on which the honest message depends. $V\in\mathrm{Spot}_d(q)$ chooses
each query from $P$, its coins and the earlier answers; always accepts the honest trace and oracle
of every \emph{admissible} instance (one consistent with $Z$); and makes in expectation at most $q$
queries that meet the target layer. It \emph{touches} neuron $u$ if some query has $u\in S_\sigma$.
\end{definition}

\noindent Row-wise openings have $d=1$ if they hide unopened rows (Remark~\ref{rem:merkle}).
Folded rows, the code columns of Section~\ref{sec:defence} and succinct arguments over the layer
have support $[N]$, so SNARK-assisted verifiers fall outside every class with $d<N$. We measure $q$
under the reference oracle $R_p$ of Definition~\ref{def:H}, which matches the real run until the
edited neuron is touched.

\para{Honest recomputation hides the edit.} The forged trace is the \emph{honest} trace of an
admissible model that differs in row $u$ alone (for a ReLU neuron: zero weights and bias $v$, or
$-1$ if $v=0$); such a forgery is \emph{explainable}.

\begin{theorem}[spot-checking is guessing the location]
\label{thm:guess}
Let $J$ be the neuron that the adversary edits, and suppose that the rows other than $J$ carry no
information about $J$ beyond the view (condition~(H) of Definition~\ref{def:H}; it holds, for
example, when the rows are independent and the adversary chooses $J$ from the values $h$ alone).
Then every $V\in\mathrm{Spot}_d(q)$ rejects an explainable single-site forgery with probability at
most
\[
  G_{qd}(J\mid P) \;=\; \mathbb E_{p}\Big[\max_{\substack{x\in[0,1]^N\\ \sum_ux_u\le qd}}\ \sum_ux_u\Pr[J=u\mid P=p]\Big],
\]
which for integer $qd$ is the probability of guessing $J$ in $qd$ tries from the view alone. For
$d=1$ and integer $q$ the bound is attained by a verifier that knows the adversary's strategy and
opens the $q$ neurons most likely to have been edited.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Until a query touches $J$, the answers equal those under the explanation, so $V$ runs as on an
honest admissible instance and accepts. By~(H), the probability $x_u$ that $V$ touches $u$ does
not depend on whether $J=u$, and $\sum_u x_u\le qd$. Detection is thus a fractional knapsack worth
at most $G_{qd}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:attack}).
\end{proof}

\noindent Condition~(H) is needed: a row that stores the location of the
critical neuron lets two adaptive queries catch the edit (Remark~\ref{rem:pointer}). For trained
networks, whose rows are correlated, we rely on the worst case (Section~\ref{sec:dichotomy}).
With independent rows, reading values helps only against improbable values or a concentrated
choice of neuron (Corollary~\ref{cor:surprise}).
On the MNIST MLP the unconstrained single-site edit is of the first kind: ranking the neurons of the
edited layer by the distance of the claimed value from the neuron's held-out mean places the edited
neuron first for \LocatorOne{}\% and \LocatorTwo{}\% of \LocatorQueries{} test queries in the two hidden layers, against $1/N$ of 0.2\%
and 0.4\%.

\subsection{Lower and Upper Bounds}
\label{sec:dichotomy}

On a suitable network (H) holds by construction, which gives a lower bound for every
spot-checking verifier.

\begin{theorem}[lower and upper bound]
\label{thm:dich}
Consider verifiers that read each layer's weights through a commitment of locality $d$ that
reveals nothing about unopened rows and keep no state between proofs, and let $q$ be the expected
number of queries that meet a layer of width $N$.
(a)~\emph{Spot-checking.} For every such verifier there is a ReLU network with a hidden layer of
width $N$, and a single-site forgery with every value in its natural range, that the verifier
accepts with probability at least $1-qd/N$, even if it knows the later weights, every neuron's
natural range and the natural distribution of each neuron of that layer. In particular, a verifier
that spot-checks $q$ of the $N$ sites of an unencoded trace ($d=1$) accepts with probability at
least $1-q/N$, whatever its sampler, and soundness error at most $\varepsilon$ on every such
network requires $qd\ge(1-\varepsilon)N$.
(b)~\emph{Mixing.} For the verifier of Section~\ref{sec:defence}, whose folded rows and code columns
each mix all rows ($d=N$), under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:amp} and $n\ge2(k-1)$,
$O(\lambda+\log L)$ queries per layer give soundness error $2^{-\lambda}$ for a network of $L$
weight layers. For $L\le2^{\lambda}$ this is $O(\lambda)$, so $qd=O(\lambda)N$ suffices.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
For~(a), hidden neuron $i$ copies input bit $\sigma(i)$ for a uniform permutation $\sigma$, and
the output is the AND of the hidden neurons. On a natural input with its one zero at $\zeta$,
setting the neuron that copies $\zeta$ to $1$ flips the output within the range $\{0,1\}$. Nothing in
the view depends on $\sigma$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:guess} bounds detection by $qd/N$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:worst}). Part~(b) is our defence's soundness argument for one layer
(Proposition~\ref{prop:amp}).
\end{proof}

\para{What the bounds mean.} In the worst case nothing beats uniform sampling, which reaches $s/N$
on every dense network; $2^{-40}$ then takes about $27.7N$ paths per query. We do not claim that
the instance of~(a) embeds in a transformer (Remark~\ref{rem:scope}). Both bounds hold at every
locality: opening $q$ uniform blocks of $d$ rows attains the bound $1-qd/N$ of~(a) ($d\mid N$) but
downloads the rows, while the checks of Section~\ref{sec:defence}, run on each block of $d$ rows encoded and
committed on its own, keep the error of $d=N$ with $O(\lambda+\log L)$ queries per block, counting
one per folded row and per opened column~\cite{fullversion}. Each answer is then a folded row or a
block column, not $d$ rows of weights.

\subsection{Measured Attacks}
\label{sec:break}

We attacked our implementation of the path protocol on an MNIST MLP (784--512--256--10), LeNet-5,
VGG-11, VGG-16 and ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, a 224$\times$224 dog-vs-cat ResNet-18 that stands in for
the unreleased model of~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}, and the real OPT-6.7B, computing path
acceptance exactly. The implementation reproduces the published behaviour~\cite{fullversion}.

\para{MNIST.} One tampered neuron passes with the predicted probability $1-1/N$ (\MnistSinglePass{}\%);
the substitute models of Anchuri et al.\ corrupt hundreds of the MLP's 778 trace nodes, our
forgeries \MnistForgeryNodes{}. A backdoor triggered by a $3{\times}3$ patch leaves clean queries unchanged,
succeeds on every triggered query not already in the target class, and passed \MnistBackdoorPass{}\% of \MnistBackdoorRuns{}
end-to-end runs (predicted: \MnistBackdoorPredicted{}\%).

\para{Other samplers.} Uniform sampling detects \SamplerUniform{}\% of attacks. The contribution-weighted
sampler, which steps to parent $i$ with probability $\propto|w_{ij}a_i|$, detects \SamplerContribution{}\% of
the best single-neuron edits but never visits a neuron whose claimed activation is zero:
zeroing about \ZeroingNeurons{} of the 512 neurons flips the prediction and passed \ZeroingAccepts{} of \ZeroingRuns{} runs. A
floor $\delta$, sampling by $|w_{ij}|(|a_i|+\delta)$, reaches \FloorBest{}\% at $\delta=1$. Each figure, the
detection of the best attack we found against that sampler, bounds its worst case from above;
\FloorBest{}\% is the best such bound among our samplers. Against an adversary that knows the sampler, keeps
every edited value within held-out ranges and adapts its edit, the best per-path mixture of the
implementable samplers, found by linear programming with a double oracle, detects at most \MixtureLayerOne{}\% and
\MixtureLayerTwo{}\% per path in the two hidden layers, below the $27.7/N$ (5.4\% and 10.8\%) at which $2^{-40}$
needs fewer than $N$ paths.

\para{Larger models.} On every CNN and test query, including the binary dog-vs-cat ResNet-18, one
changed penultimate neuron flips the prediction and is caught by a path with probability \CnnPathLenet{}
(LeNet-5) to \CnnPathMax{} (VGG, ResNet). $2^{-40}$ takes
\CnnPathsRange{} paths, which open the whole model and trace, and at least \PathsLlama{} in the FFN
layers of Llama-2-7B (width \WidthLlama{}), about \OpenAllLlama{}\,GB of fp16 openings at 64 tokens.

\para{A real LLM.} On OPT-6.7B (fp16), one FFN neuron of layer 8, 16, 24 or 31 changed the next
token for \AttackOptLSucceeded{} of \AttackOptLPrompts{} prompts (95\% Wilson interval for the success rate: \AttackOptLWilson{}\%). A fixed-target backdoor, constrained by the final LayerNorm, succeeds
for ``\texttt{back}'' (\AttackOptLBackdoorBack{}, clean prompts unchanged) and fails for ``\texttt{hacked}'' (\AttackOptLBackdoorHacked{}).
Its values exceed the natural ranges, which the path protocol does not check, and $2^{-40}$ needs
at least \AttackOptLPaths{} paths. Range checks do not stop the edit. Against per-neuron ranges calibrated on
held-out text (\InrangeCalibTokens{} tokens of WikiText-2 train), edits that keep every value in range change the
next token of OPT-1.3B for \InrangeChanged{} prompts at each of layers 6, 12, 18 and 23, with a median of \InrangeKMedian{}
neurons (at most \InrangeKMax{}); a path opens them with median probability at most \InrangePMedianMax{} in every
cell (at most \InrangePMax{}). Edits that only lower activations, which a contribution-weighted
sampler of the claimed trace never opens, succeed for \InrangeDown{} of the \InrangePrompts{} prompts at those
layers, with a median of \InrangeDownKMedian{} neurons. Where they fail, value-aware sampling helps against these
edits, which are not adapted to the sampler: with the natural maximum as the range, the best
per-path mixture of our samplers catches the better of the two edits on \GameLayerSix{}\%, \GameLayerTwelve{}\% and \GameLayerEighteen{}\% of
the paths that enter the FFN of layers 6, 12 and 18 (mean over prompts), above $27.7/N$ (\GameThreshold{}\%),
and on \GameLayerTwentyThree{}\% at layer 23 (uniform: \GameUniform{}\%).
Whether an edit adapted to the sampler evades it, as on the MLP, we leave open. On OPT-6.7B
(layers 8, 16, 24 and 31), in-range edits change the next
token for \InrangeOptLChanged{} prompts at every layer, and a path opens them with median probability at most
\InrangeOptLPMedianMax{}.

A single-site edit is caught exactly when the sample covers it (Table~\ref{tab:sok}), and no
other value-blind sample of the same size does better against every edit placement
(Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}). SLP, CommitLLM, NanoZK and SPEX state probabilistic guarantees,
and SLP and CommitLLM describe the edit-then-recompute strategy themselves~\cite{slp,commitllm2026};
we do not call them broken. The path protocol's full soundness for binary classifiers fails on our
dog-vs-cat ResNet-18. TensorCommitments' stated goal of negligible soundness is not achieved by its
layer selection, which the provider can compute. Selection by value is evaded by edits that write
common values (our zeroing attack; blindfolded updates~\cite{fang2023pol}).
