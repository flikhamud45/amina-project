\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Open-weight language models are often served by a provider the client does not control. The provider
may serve a cheaper model, or alter the computation on chosen queries to return answers of its
choice. We target a client that has no GPU, does not store the weights, and holds only a 32-byte
root per weight matrix (mode~$\sC$) or a small secret key precomputed once from the weights
(mode~$\Kpre$). In a \emph{proof of inference}, the provider sends a proof with each answer, and
the client's check fails with high probability unless the agreed model computed the answer on this
input. The agreed model is an exact int8 network derived from the public
fp16 checkpoint by a deterministic, public quantisation recipe with fixed calibration data, so
anyone can recompute its commitment.

zkSNARK-based proofs~\cite{zkcnn,zkml,ezkl,zkllm,zkgpt,deepprove,zktorch} give this guarantee with
small proofs, but proving costs far more than inference: zkLLM needs about ten minutes on an A100
for one 2{,}048-token Llama-2-7B prompt~\cite{zkllm}. Trusted execution
environments~\cite{slalom,twinshield,veriattn,gpucc} are fast but trust the hardware vendor. Lighter
protocols check only a sample of the computation. In the path protocol of Anchuri et
al.~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}, the provider commits to the execution trace with a Merkle tree,
and the client re-checks one random output-to-input path against a commitment to the weights. Other
designs sample chunks~\cite{slp}, layers~\cite{tensorcommitments,nanozk,commitllm2026} or
requests~\cite{spex,immaculate}. A different model leaves many inconsistent sites, so a sample is
likely to hit one. Anchuri et al.\ prove this \emph{other-model} soundness, note that one path
catches a single changed node with probability at most $1/N$ in a layer of width $N$, and leave
\emph{full} soundness, against any wrong answer, open for large models.

\para{The attack.} The provider needs no other model: it overwrites the value of one neuron and
recomputes every later layer honestly with the true weights. The trace is then inconsistent at one
site, which a random path visits with probability $1/N$ (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). On the real
OPT-6.7B, editing one feed-forward neuron changed the next token for 40 of 40 prompts, with values
outside the neurons' natural ranges; on OPT-1.3B, editing a few neurons within ranges calibrated on
held-out text does the same. On triggered inputs only, it is a backdoor that leaves the
committed weights clean. On a dog-vs-cat ResNet-18, one changed neuron flips every test query and
passes a path with probability $511/512$, refuting the full soundness that Anchuri et al.\ state
for binary classifiers. More paths do not help at a useful cost: detection with probability
$1-2^{-40}$ on Llama-2-7B takes about 305{,}000 paths.

\para{A lower bound for spot-checking.} After honest recomputation, the forged trace is the honest
trace of a model that differs from the committed one in one weight row. Consider a verifier that
reads the weights through a commitment whose queried symbols each depend on at most $d$ rows,
chooses its queries from the whole claimed trace, and treats prover messages such as folded rows as
further queries (verifiers assisted by a succinct argument are excluded). If the other rows carry no
information about the edit's location, a verifier with $q$ queries per layer rejects the forgery at
most as often as it could guess the edited site in $qd$ tries (Theorem~\ref{thm:guess}). For every
such verifier, some ReLU network with a hidden layer of width $N$ admits a single-neuron forgery,
with every value in its natural range, that passes with probability at least $1-qd/N$. Conversely,
symbols that mix all rows, such as Reed--Solomon columns, reach $2^{-\lambda}$ with $O(\lambda)$
queries per layer (Theorem~\ref{thm:dich}); intermediate locality is open. This formalises a
folklore calculation~\cite{juels2007por,ergun2000spotcheckers,bghsv2006robust} for adaptive,
value-aware verifiers. Unlike the verifiers of PCPs of proximity and holographic proofs, ours reads
the whole trace, which does not help: the model, not the trace, must be encoded. Of nine published
schemes for verifying inference (Table~\ref{tab:sok}), most meet the probabilistic guarantees they state; we
do not call them broken. Apart from the binary-classifier claim, the only gap is in
TensorCommitments~\cite{tensorcommitments}: its stated goal of negligible soundness is not achieved
by its layer-selection rule, which depends only on the weights. We disclosed these findings to the
authors \todo{only once the notices of 15--19 Oct are sent; else delete}.

\para{A defence that checks everything.} Since no verifier beats uniform sampling in the worst case,
ours checks every weight operation. The provider runs the exact int8 model, bit-identical on its GPU
and on the client, and sends every weight operation's result. The verifier recomputes every
operation without weights, including attention, from checked values, and tests each weight operation
with random linear combinations of its output rows (Freivalds' algorithm~\cite{freivalds1977}). In
mode~$\sC$ it checks each combination against a Reed--Solomon-encoded, Merkle-committed copy of the
weights. This is the evaluation protocol of a linear-code polynomial commitment, as in Ligero and
Brakedown~\cite{ligero,brakedown}, applied to every matrix product of the model. A tampered value
anywhere is caught except with probability $2^{-\lambda}$, using only a hash function and a linear
code: no SNARK, trusted hardware or trusted setup.

We add four extensions. First, a committer can bind a non-codeword matrix that answers for two
models, which is why such commitments have a testing phase. When the client cannot recompute the
commitment, we amortise this phase into a one-time setup proof (Section~\ref{sec:setup-proof}).
Second, the Fiat--Shamir transcript binds every constant of the computation, avoiding the weak
Fiat--Shamir pattern~\cite{bernhard2012pitfalls,dao2023weakfs}. Third, a generated response is
proved as one prefill, and each token is checked as the greedy choice or as that of an exact integer
sampler keyed by the client's seed, at no extra soundness cost (Section~\ref{sec:gen}). Fourth, in
V1 the provider sends the int8 value that the next operation reads instead of each 32-bit result,
and a logUp-GKR~\cite{haboeck2022logup,papini2023logupgkr} shows that each hidden result lies in
that value's requantisation window. These conditions are linear in the committed weights, so V1
reuses the column check and adds no commitment (Section~\ref{sec:v1}).

\para{Costs.} Without V1, the prover's work beyond the forward pass does not grow with the prompt.
On an L40S it proves a 2{,}048-token Llama-2-13B prompt 46$\times$ faster than zkLLM on an A100,
although zkLLM also hides the weights and proves the operations without weights \todo{L40S: final
factor; overhead over unverified inference}. The main cost is the proof, which carries every weight
operation's result: 6.6\,GB for a 2{,}048-token Llama-2-7B prompt, about the size of its int8
weights. V1 makes it 2.0--2.3$\times$ smaller with random weights and 1.7--1.8$\times$ on the real
OPT-125M at 512 tokens, but slows the prover. With native exact kernels, a CPU client in
mode~$\Kpre$ verifies a 2{,}048-token Llama-2-7B prompt 4.6$\times$ faster than re-executing it in
fp32 on the same CPU; on a GPU, re-execution is faster \todo{L40S/EPYC: full models}. The protocol is not zero-knowledge, so it targets open-weight models, as do
CommitLLM~\cite{commitllm2026} and Gupta et al.~\cite{gupta2026private}.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} compares a client's options.

\para{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An attack on spot-checking proofs of inference, a single-site edit followed by honest
  recomputation, measured on image classifiers and OPT-6.7B (Section~\ref{sec:attack}).
  \item A lower bound for spot-checking verifiers with a matching upper bound at full locality,
  applied to nine published schemes (Sections~\ref{sec:sampling}--\ref{sec:sok}).
  \item A fully sound Freivalds-based protocol that needs only a hash function and a linear code,
  with untrusted commitments, binding Fiat--Shamir, generation and V1
  (Sections~\ref{sec:defence}--\ref{sec:gen}).
  \item An evaluation on language models up to 13B parameters, against re-execution and published
  systems (Section~\ref{sec:results}) \todo{L40S}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\para{zkSNARK proofs of inference.} Sum-check and GKR provers~\cite{gkr,zkcnn,zkpytorch,verfcnn},
pairing- and Plonk-based systems~\cite{vcnn,zen,zeno,zkml,ezkl,bionetta} and the VOLE-based
Mystique~\cite{mystique} prove CNNs. zkLLM~\cite{zkllm}, zkGPT~\cite{zkgpt},
DeepProve~\cite{deepprove}, ZKTorch~\cite{zktorch}, Jolt Atlas~\cite{joltatlas} and NanoZK's full
mode~\cite{nanozk} prove language models. They give computational soundness with proofs of under a
kilobyte to tens of megabytes, and many hide the weights, but their provers are slow
(Table~\ref{tab:eval-compare}). DeepProve and zkAgent~\cite{zkagent} prove a generated sequence in
one pass, as we do. V1 borrows the logUp-GKR~\cite{papini2023logupgkr} that such systems use, but
commits no activation.

\para{Spot-checking and sampling.} Table~\ref{tab:sok} describes the sampling schemes that our
theorem
covers~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable,slp,commitllm2026,tensorcommitments,nanozk,spex,immaculate,toploc,svip}.
Optimistic schemes~\cite{verde,opml,tao} accept an answer unless a party that re-runs the model
disputes it, so they need an honest party that re-runs every query. The calculation behind the
edit-then-recompute attack is classical. It underlies proofs of retrievability and data
possession~\cite{juels2007por,ateniese2007pdp}, spot-checkers~\cite{ergun2000spotcheckers} and PCPs
of proximity, which certify only closeness, whereas holographic proofs encode their
input~\cite{bghsv2006robust,bfls1991,chiesa2020fractal}. Its tight case is the search for a hidden
object~\cite{chew1967,kadane1971}.

\para{Trusted hardware.} Slalom~\cite{slalom} checks the linear layers of a GPU computation from an
SGX enclave with Freivalds' algorithm. TwinShield~\cite{twinshield} and VeriAttn~\cite{veriattn}
extend enclave-based checking to attention, VeriAttn within a tolerance. GPU confidential computing
runs the whole model inside an attested H100-class GPU, with below 7\% overhead for most LLM
queries~\cite{gpucc}, but trusts the vendor's hardware. We need no trusted hardware, and exact integer semantics leaves no tolerance in which to hide a change.

\para{Freivalds' check and linear-code commitments.} SafetyNets~\cite{safetynets} verifies networks
with quadratic activations by an interactive proof, and ZKML~\cite{zkml} uses Freivalds' check
inside a SNARK. Maverick~\cite{maverick} checks every linear layer of a language model with sparse
Freivalds checks against a client-stored encoded copy of the weights. Gupta et
al.~\cite{gupta2026private} run Freivalds' check in a group's exponent against data that the model
owner generates with secret randomness, for two non-colluding servers.
CommitLLM~\cite{commitllm2026} checks int8 models with secret Freivalds vectors precomputed from the
weights, at 12--14\% serving overhead, and audits attention without verifying it. LAMP~\cite{lamp}
checks a matrix product against a commitment with Freivalds' check and Reed--Solomon proximity
testing inside a Groth16 SNARK. DeepBrake~\cite{deepbrake} merges the testing and evaluation phases
of a row-wise Reed--Solomon commitment into one proximity test for points fixed in advance;
ReedWeave~\cite{reedweave} and BaseFold~\cite{basefold} fold codewords for faster openings. Such
tests could shorten our setup proof.

\para{Where we fit.} We keep the client of Anchuri et al., which holds only a weight commitment, and
replace sampling, which needs $(1-\varepsilon)N$ paths per layer for soundness $1-\varepsilon$, with
a check of every weight operation. Each fully sound protocol above that avoids a SNARK needs more
than a commitment: trusted hardware, a stored encoded copy of the weights, a weight-derived secret
or data generated with secret randomness. To our knowledge, ours is the first Freivalds-based proof
of inference that is fully sound for complete transformers, with attention recomputed by the verifier from checked values, whose
client (in mode~$\sC$) needs none of these; the novelty is this combination, not a new primitive.
