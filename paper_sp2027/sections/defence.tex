\section{The Defence: Checking Every Weight Operation}
\label{sec:defence}

Theorem~\ref{thm:dich} leaves one way to reach soundness $2^{-\lambda}$ well below the cost of
re-execution: the verifier must touch every row of every weight matrix, through checks that each
mix many rows. Our defence tests random linear combinations of all the neurons of every weight
operation with Freivalds' algorithm~\cite{freivalds1977}; a client without the weights checks each
combination against a Reed--Solomon-encoded, Merkle-committed copy of them, as in Ligero and
Brakedown~\cite{ligero,brakedown}. The
provider (the prover) answers each query $x$ with an output $y$ and a proof and may deviate
arbitrarily; full soundness requires that every $y\neq M(x)$ be rejected except with probability
$\varepsilon$. In mode $\sC$ the client (the verifier) holds only a commitment $C_M$ to the
weights, as in the path protocol~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}. In mode $\sK$ it holds the weights,
and in mode $\Kpre$ also secret Freivalds vectors and their folded rows, precomputed once per model
as in Slalom~\cite{slalom} and CommitLLM~\cite{commitllm2026}. Appendix~\ref{app:defence} contains
the proofs.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Exact Integer Model}
\label{sec:intmodel}

The checks compare exact values. Floating-point results depend on the device and the summation
order, and a tolerance would leave a cheating prover room to hide small changes. We therefore
commit and check an integer network obtained by integer-only quantisation~\cite{jacob2018quant},
as proof systems and enclave-based checks for inference also do~\cite{safetynets,slalom,zkcnn,zkml,zkllm}.
Soundness refers to this integer model, not to the floating-point checkpoint;
Section~\ref{sec:quality} measures the gap between the two.

\para{Weight operations.} A \emph{weight operation} $\ell$ of the public graph computes
\begin{equation}
  Z_\ell = A_\ell\,[X_\ell;\mathbf 1], \qquad A_\ell=[W_\ell\,|\,b_\ell],
  \label{eq:weightop}
\end{equation}
where $W_\ell$ is an int8 matrix with $N_\ell$ rows, $b_\ell$ an integer bias, and $X_\ell$ an int8
matrix with one column for each of its $T_\ell$ positions (pixels or tokens). We write $k_\ell$ for
the row length of $A_\ell$. Convolutions become products through im2col, BatchNorm is folded into
$W_\ell$ and $b_\ell$, and the scales of the weights are absorbed into the integer multiplier of the
next requantisation. Embedding tables are looked up.

\para{Operations without weights.} The other operations map integers to integers with public
constants fixed at calibration. A requantisation with multiplier $m$ computes
$\mathrm{clamp}(\lfloor(mz+2^{29})/2^{30}\rfloor,-127,127)$. The residual stream holds 23-bit
integers, norms use an integer square root, activation functions and the exponential in softmax
are tables of 256 entries, and softmax outputs 8-bit probabilities (Appendix~\ref{app:semantics}).
The output $y$ is the claim of the last weight operation. For a language model these are the
next-token logits, which depend only on the last position, so the last block is computed there
alone, except for the keys and values that attention reads. Section~\ref{sec:gen} extends the
statement to generated responses.

\para{Exact execution.} Integer results do not depend on the order of summation, so prover and
verifier obtain the same integers on any hardware as long as nothing is rounded. Every weight
operation's input is int8 (it comes from a clamped requantisation, table or norm), so the prover
runs $W_\ell X_\ell$ on int8 tensor cores: products are at most $2^{14}$ in magnitude, and blocks
of $2^{16}$ of them sum exactly in 32-bit integers. Attention runs in float32 within its exact range
(Appendix~\ref{app:semantics}). A hardware error can only make an honest proof fail.

\para{Embedding in a field.} The checks run in the BabyBear field $\F$, $p=15\cdot2^{27}+1\approx
2^{31}$. The verifier rejects every claim with $|z|\ge2^{29}$, and setup checks that every honest
claim is smaller: $128\sum_c|w_{ic}|+|b_i|<2^{29}$ for every row $i$ of a product, and
$\max_c|w_{ic}|+|b_i|<2^{29}$ for an embedding. A wrong claim and the correct value then differ by
less than $2^{30}<p$, so they remain distinct in $\F$.

\subsection{Committing to the Weights}
\label{sec:commit}

At setup, every row of $A_\ell$, a message of length $k_\ell$, is Reed--Solomon
encoded~\cite{reedsolomon1960}: the polynomial whose coefficients are the row is evaluated at the
$n\ge k_\ell$ points of a multiplicative subgroup of $\F$, with $n$ a power of two. The codewords form the
rows of $\hat A_\ell$. Encoding is linear, so $\chi^\T\hat A_\ell=\enc(\chi^\T A_\ell)$, and two
distinct codewords agree on at most $k_\ell-1$ positions. A Merkle tree~\cite{merkle1987} binds the
columns of $\hat A_\ell$; we use SHA-256 with separate domain prefixes for leaves and inner nodes.
$C_M$ consists of the roots and the public parameters of the encoding, 32 bytes per tree. It uses
no secret randomness, and anyone who holds the weights can recompute it.

\para{Commitment plans.} Long codewords need fewer opened columns, and matrices whose codewords
have equal length can share one tree and one Merkle multiproof. A public planning rule picks,
among six candidate plans of codeword lengths and layouts, the one that sends the fewest bytes per
query besides the claims, within a cap on the size of the setup; the plan is part of $C_M$. The
five weight matrices of LeNet-5, for example, share one tree of $2^{18}$ positions and open 15
columns, instead of 66 in each of five trees. For a layer with many rows, such as GPT-2's output
layer ($N=50{,}257$), the rows of $A_\ell^\T$ are encoded instead (the \emph{transposed layout}),
and operations that read the same input, such as the query, key and value projections, are
stacked into one such matrix. An embedding table without a bias is committed as a Merkle tree over
its rows, as in CommitLLM~\cite{commitllm2026}.

\subsection{The Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

% The course paper's message diagram (figures/protocol.pdf), restored at assembly.
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/protocol.pdf}
  \caption{One query in mode $\sC$, with an honestly computed commitment. Each value is fixed
  before the randomness that tests it}
  \label{fig:protocol}
\end{figure}
With $y$, the prover sends the \emph{claims} $Z_\ell$ of every weight operation; $y$ is itself a
claim. All claims are correct if and only if three statements hold for every weight operation
$\ell$: (S1)~$X_\ell$ follows from $x$ and the earlier claims through the operations without
weights; (S2)~$Z_\ell=A_\ell[X_\ell;\mathbf 1]$; and (S3)~$A_\ell$ is the committed matrix. In mode
$\sC$ a query has three rounds (Figure~\ref{fig:protocol}): the claims, then the vectors $\chi$ and the folded rows, then the
column positions and the opened columns. Each value is fixed before the randomness that tests it.

\para{(S1): recomputation.} The verifier computes every operation without weights itself, so each
$X_\ell$ is its own value and the checks are chained: a prover cannot pass one operation's check
with one value and feed another into the next. Non-linear operations, softmax and the attention
products need no proof.

\para{(S2): Freivalds' check.} After all claims are fixed, the verifier draws a uniform
$\chi_\ell\in\F^{N_\ell\times r}$ and checks
\begin{equation}
  \chi_\ell^\T Z_\ell \;=\; u_\ell\,[X_\ell;\mathbf 1], \qquad u_\ell=\chi_\ell^\T A_\ell,
  \label{eq:freivalds}
\end{equation}
where we call $u_\ell$ the \emph{folded rows}. The two sides differ by $\chi_\ell^\T D$, where
$D=Z_\ell-A_\ell[X_\ell;\mathbf 1]$. If $D_{ij}\neq0$, then for each column $\chi$ of $\chi_\ell$,
entry $j$ of $\chi^\T D$ is $\chi_iD_{ij}+c$, where $c$ does not involve $\chi_i$, and it vanishes
for one of the $p$ values of $\chi_i$. A wrong operation thus passes with probability at most
$p^{-r}$, however many of its values are wrong: one tampered neuron is caught as reliably as a
wrong layer. Given $u_\ell$, the check costs $O(r(N_\ell+k_\ell)T_\ell)$, against
$O(N_\ell k_\ell T_\ell)$ for recomputing the operation.

\para{(S3): the column check.} In modes $\sK$ and $\Kpre$ the verifier computes $u_\ell$ itself. In
mode $\sC$ the prover sends $u_\ell$. Opening a few of its entries would miss a single wrong one, as
a path misses a single neuron, so the verifier tests $u_\ell$ through the code: it draws $t$
distinct uniform positions $c$, receives the columns $\hat A_{\ell,c}$ with a Merkle multiproof, and
checks the paths and $\chi_\ell^\T\hat A_{\ell,c}=\enc(u_\ell)_c$. If $u_\ell\neq\chi_\ell^\T A_\ell$, some row of
$\enc(u_\ell)$ and the same row of $\chi_\ell^\T\hat A_\ell$ are distinct codewords, so all $t$
columns fall where they agree with probability at most $\prod_{i<t}(k_\ell-1-i)/(n-i)<(k_\ell/n)^t$. A
transposed matrix needs no folded row: the verifier combines positions instead of neurons, with
$\chi'\in\F^{T\times r}$, and checks the encoding of $Z\chi'$ against the opened columns combined by
$[X;\mathbf 1]\chi'$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sound}). The opened rows of a lookup table are checked
against its root.

\para{Modes $\sK$ and $\Kpre$.} In $\sK$ the verifier draws a fresh $\chi_\ell$ for each query and
computes $u_\ell$ itself, so the proof is the claims alone. In $\Kpre$ it computes the secret
$\chi_\ell$ and $u_\ell$ once per model. This is a designated-verifier mode: nobody else can check
its proofs, and soundness is lost if $\chi_\ell$ leaks.

\para{Costs.} The prover's extra work, $O((r+t)\sum_\ell N_\ell k_\ell)$, does not depend on the
number of positions, and no term of the verifier's work grows with $N_\ell k_\ell T_\ell$. The
proof holds the claims, which grow with $T$, and in mode $\sC$ the folded rows, opened columns and
Merkle paths, which do not. Section~\ref{sec:v1} replaces most claims by the int8 values that their
consumers read, reusing the folds and the column check.

\para{Soundness.} Let $\mathcal J$ be the set of committed matrices: one for each weight operation
in the row layout and one for each transposed matrix. Matrix $j$ has message length $k_j$ and
codeword length $n_j$, and its tree opens $t_j$ columns.

\begin{theorem}[soundness]
\label{thm:sound}
Suppose that $C_M$ is computed honestly from the weights of $M$ and that the prover finds no hash
collision. For every input $x$, which the prover may choose, and every prover, the verifier of mode
$\sC$ accepts an output $y\neq M(x)$ with probability at most
\[
  \varepsilon_{\sC}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\Big[\,p^{-r}+\prod_{i<t_j}\frac{k_j-1-i}{n_j-i}\,\Big].
\]
In mode $\sK$ the bound is $\varepsilon_{\sK}=\sum_\ell p^{-r}$, over the weight operations that
are not looked up. In mode $\Kpre$, over $Q$ queries that reuse the secret vectors, the verifier
accepts some $y\neq M(x)$ with probability at most $Q\,\varepsilon_{\sK}$, even if the prover learns
which check failed in every rejected query.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} The first wrong weight operation has a correct input, which the
verifier computed from correct claims. With the true $u_\ell$ Freivalds' check fails except with
probability $p^{-r}$, and with another $u_\ell$ the column check fails except with the column term.
In $\Kpre$, until a wrong claim passes, every outcome is a function of the prover's messages, so
its view does not depend on $\chi_\ell$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sound}). \qed

\para{Parameters.} We set $\beta=\lambda+\log_2(2|\mathcal J|)$ and $r=\lceil\beta/\log_2p\rceil$,
and each tree opens the least $t$ that keeps the column term of every member below $2^{-\beta}$.
Then $\varepsilon_{\sC}\le2^{-\lambda}$; at $\lambda=128$, $r=5$. Each folded row and each column
mixes all rows of its matrix, so the
protocol is in the second regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:dich}: it touches every row, at a cost of
$O(\lambda)$ field elements per row instead of $k_\ell$ weights.

\subsection{Non-Interactive Proofs}
\label{sec:fs}

Interactively, each challenge is expanded with SHAKE-256~\cite{fips202} from a fresh 256-bit key
that the verifier draws. Under Fiat--Shamir~\cite{fiatshamir1986} the keys are derived from the
transcript instead, and two details of this derivation matter for soundness.

\para{The statement binds the computation.} Challenges that do not depend on the whole statement
make one transcript valid for several statements, the weak Fiat--Shamir pattern that has broken
deployed proof systems~\cite{bernhard2012pitfalls,dao2023weakfs}. Our verifier recomputes the
operations without weights, so their constants belong to the statement. The transcript therefore
starts with a \emph{graph digest}, the SHA-256 hash of a canonical description of the graph: every
operation in order with its inputs and output, the shape, layout and input bound of each weight
operation, and the function of each other operation with every constant it uses, table bytes
included. It then absorbs the security parameters, the commitment, the input $x$ and, for sampled
generation, the sampler's parameters and the client's seed (Section~\ref{sec:gen}). Two models
that differ in one requantisation multiplier thus produce different challenges.

\para{A sponge transcript.} The transcript is a SHAKE-256 sponge with a protocol-specific domain
string, and messages are absorbed with labels and length prefixes, so its encoding is injective.
A challenge key is 32 bytes squeezed from a copy of the sponge after it absorbs the challenge's
label; rejection sampling expands it into unbiased field elements and distinct column indices. The
challenges are thus random-oracle outputs on prefix-free inputs, which a Merkle--Damg\aa{}rd hash
such as SHA-256 would give only with a separate argument~\cite{coron2005md}. The Merkle trees keep
SHA-256, from which they need only collision resistance.

\para{Soundness.} The interactive protocol is round-by-round sound~\cite{canetti2019fs}: once a
wrong claim is fixed, the vectors $\chi$ rescue the prover with probability at most $p^{-r}$, and
once a wrong folded row is fixed, the columns rescue it with probability at most its column term.
It follows~\cite{canetti2019fs,holmgren2019rbr,bcs16} that a prover making $Q$ random-oracle
queries, for example to rehash altered claims in search of favourable challenges, succeeds with
probability at most $Q\varepsilon_{\sC}+O(Q^22^{-256})$. We add 64 bits to $\beta$, which keeps
$Q\varepsilon_{\sC}\le2^{-\lambda}$ for $Q\le2^{64}$ and raises $r$ to 7 at $\lambda=128$. The
encoding of the claims is not canonical, but each re-encoding of the same claims costs one oracle
query, which the bound already counts.

\subsection{Untrusted Commitments}
\label{sec:setup-proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} assumes that $C_M$ is honest, as the path protocol
does~\cite{anchuri2026verifiable}. This holds by verification when the client can recompute $C_M$,
for instance from the published int8 checkpoint of an open-weight model. When the provider
commits, the per-query column check does not enforce it.

\para{The split-commitment attack.} A committer can bind any matrix $E_j$ under a root, not only a
matrix of codewords. Let $A$ and $A'$ be two models that differ in matrix $j$, for example by a
backdoor. The committer binds the encoded columns of $A_j$ on a secret half of the positions of
$j$'s tree and those of $A'_j$ on the other half. A prover that computes with $A'$ sends
$u=\chi^\T A'_j$, passes Freivalds' check, and passes the column check whenever all $t$ opened
columns fall into the half of $A'_j$:
\begin{equation}
  \Pr[\text{accept}] \;\ge\; \prod_{i<t}\frac{n/2-i}{n-i}\;\approx\; 2^{-t}.
  \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
For the smallest tree of Llama-2-7B ($t=35$) this is about $2^{-35}$ per query, not $2^{-128}$. On a
small decoder with one weight row changed in one operation, the acceptance rate over 300 queries
with a small $t$ matched Eq.~\eqref{eq:split}. Against a malformed commitment a column is worth
about one bit instead of $\log_2(n_j/k_j)$, so per-query checks alone would need 3.2--6.6$\times$ the
column bytes on the models of Table~\ref{tab:setup}.

\para{One combination over an extension field.} In a 31-bit field, a random combination of the
rows of a malformed matrix lands close to the code with probability up to about $n/p$. The
protocol, however, checks $r$ independent vectors at the same columns.

\begin{fact}
\label{fact:ext}
Let $\mathbb K_s=\F[\xi]/(f)$ for an irreducible $f$ of degree $s$, and let $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_s\in\F^R$
be independent and uniform. Then $\psi=\sum_i\xi^{i-1}\chi_i$ is uniform in $\mathbb K_s^R$. For every
$E\in\F^{R\times n}$ and set $S$ of columns, the $s$ combinations $\chi_i^\T E$ agree on $S$ with
codewords of the Reed--Solomon code over $\F$ if and only if $\psi^\T E$ agrees on $S$ with a
codeword of the Reed--Solomon code over $\mathbb K_s$ with the same evaluation points.
\end{fact}

Proximity errors therefore scale with $p^{-s}$ rather than $p^{-1}$. For the per-query vectors
($s=r=5$ at $\lambda=128$), the unique-decoding error $n/p^r$ is at most $2^{-136.6}$ for
$n\le2^{18}$. An untrusted commitment needs more columns, not more randomness.

\para{A one-time setup proof.} Once per model, the committer publishes with $C_M$ a
non-interactive proof that every committed matrix $E_j$ is \emph{$e_j$-close} to a matrix of
codewords: it differs from $\enc(A^*_j)$ on at most $e_j<(n_j-k_j+1)/2$ columns, which makes
$A^*_j$ unique. With Fiat--Shamir challenges over the graph digest and every tree's root and
shape, (1)~the verifier draws $s$ uniform base-field combinations $\psi_j$ of the rows of each
matrix; (2)~the committer sends $\psi_jA^*_j$; (3)~the verifier draws $t^0_j$ distinct uniform
columns of each tree, which the committer opens with a multiproof; and (4)~the verifier checks the
multiproofs and $\enc(\psi_jA^*_j)_c=\psi_jE_{j,c}$ (parameters in Appendix~\ref{app:untrusted}).
Transposed matrices need this proof: their per-query combination $[X;\mathbf 1]\chi'$ lies in the
span of the activations and is not uniform, so it cannot establish closeness.

\begin{theorem}[soundness with an untrusted commitment]
\label{thm:untrusted}
Model the hash functions as random oracles, and let the committer and the prover make at most $Q$
oracle queries in total. Let $C_M$ be any string and let $\pi$ be any setup proof that the verifier
accepts. Then, except with probability
\[
  \varepsilon_0\le Q\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\Big[\frac{n_j}{p^s}+\prod_{i<t^0_j}
  \frac{n_j-e_j-1-i}{n_j-i}\Big]+O(Q^22^{-256}),
\]
every matrix $E_j$ that $C_M$ binds is $e_j$-close to $\enc(A^*_j)$ for a unique $A^*_j$. Let
$M^*$ be the model with weights $A^*$ whose weight products are computed in $\F$ and lifted to
$(-p/2,p/2)$. For every input $x$ and every prover, the verifier of mode $\sC$ then accepts
$y\neq M^*(x)$ with probability at most
\[
  \varepsilon^*_{\sC}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\Big[\,p^{-r}+\prod_{i<t_j}\frac{k_j+e_j-1-i}{n_j-i}\,\Big],
\]
or $Q\varepsilon^*_{\sC}+O(Q^22^{-256})$ under Fiat--Shamir. If the client recomputes $C_M$, then
$e_j=0$, no setup proof is needed, and the bound is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} If an extracted $E_j$ is far, Fact~\ref{fact:ext} and correlated
agreement for Reed--Solomon codes in the unique-decoding regime~\cite{bciks20} make
$\psi_j^\T E_j$ far from the code over $\mathbb K_s$ except with probability $n_j/p^s$. Online, a wrong
folded row survives only on the at most $k_j-1+e_j$ positions where its codeword agrees with the
true one or where $E_j$ is corrupted, a set fixed before the query
(Appendix~\ref{app:untrusted}). \qed

\noindent $C_M$ thus fixes $M^*$ before any query, and $M^*$ is the integer model of
Section~\ref{sec:intmodel} when $A^*$ is int8 and meets the range bound. A client that sees neither
the weights nor a range proof on them learns that the provider is bound to one fixed model, but not
which one.

\para{The trade-off.} A larger radius $e_j$ shortens the setup proof, since each setup column then
catches a far matrix with probability about $e_j/n_j$, and lengthens every query, whose bound has
$k_j+e_j$ in place of $k_j$. Each tree takes the radius, among the fractions $2^0,\dots,2^{-10}$ of
the unique-decoding radius, that minimises $t^0_j/a+t_j$ over $a$ amortised queries
(Table~\ref{tab:setup}). At $a=1{,}000$, Llama-2-7B pays 11.3\,GB once and 8\% more column bytes per
query; at 2{,}048 tokens the change per query is below 1\%. Our implementation covers commitment
plans, including shared trees and transposed matrices, and rejects the setup proof of the split
commitment. \todo{L40S: setup prover and verifier time for GPT-2 and the 7B models.}

\para{Scope.} Neither the setup proof nor mode $\sC$ hides the weights: the verifier knows $X_\ell$
and $Z_\ell$, so the claims of about $\lceil k_\ell/T\rceil$ queries reveal $A_\ell$. Mode $\sC$ thus
suits weights that the client may see. When the int8 checkpoint is public, recomputing $C_M$ also
settles which model is committed, and it is cheaper than a setup proof, which for GPT-2 is larger
than the int8 weights. The setup proof serves clients that cannot recompute $C_M$.
