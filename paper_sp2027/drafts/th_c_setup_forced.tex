\begin{remark}[setup tests that open columns]
\label{rem:forced}
This remark concerns \emph{column-hash} commitments, in which a Merkle leaf binds whole columns of
the committed matrices. Ours is one (Section~\ref{sec:commit}), and so are Ligero's and
Brakedown's~\cite{ligero,brakedown}. We idealise each tree as an oracle that returns one leaf per
query. This is the model of interactive oracle proofs~\cite{bcs16}, in which FRI, STIR, WHIR and
BaseFold are analysed~\cite{fri2018,stir,whir,basefold}. A \emph{column-only} test is a verifier
whose only access to the committed matrices is through such queries. It may run any number of
rounds, draw its challenges from any field, receive further messages or oracles from the committer
and choose its queries adaptively. It is \emph{complete} if it accepts every honestly computed
$C_M$, with the honest committer, with probability~1. Let $j\in\mathcal J$ with $2e_j<n_j-k_j$, and let
\[
  P_j(t)=\prod_{i<t}\frac{n_j-e_j-1-i}{n_j-i}.
\]
(i)~For every complete column-only test that opens at most $t$ distinct leaves of $j$'s tree, some
committer binds a matrix $E_j$ that is not $e_j$-close and passes the test with probability at least
$P_j(t)$.
(ii)~Hence a complete column-only test that every such committer passes with probability at most
$2^{-\beta}$ opens at least $t^0_j(\beta)$ leaves of $j$'s tree, where $t^0_j(\beta)$ is the least $t$ with
$P_j(t)\le2^{-\beta}$. Since
\[
  \Big(1-\frac{e_j+1}{n_j-t+1}\Big)^{t}\;\le\;P_j(t)\;\le\;\Big(1-\frac{e_j+1}{n_j}\Big)^{t},
\]
$t^0_j(\beta)$ is about $\beta/{-\log_2(1-e_j/n_j)}$ when $t^0_j(\beta)\ll n_j$.
(iii)~In each tree, our setup proof opens the maximum of $t^0_j(\beta_0)$ over the tree's members
(Appendix~\ref{app:untrusted}). So at its radii and at error $2^{-\beta_0}$ per matrix, no complete
column-only test opens fewer leaves of any tree. The column term of $\varepsilon_0$ is also tight:
the committer of~(i) passes our setup proof with probability at least $P_j(t^0_j(\beta_0))$, where
$j$ is the member that attains the maximum.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard counting argument for testers with perfect completeness.
(i)~Fix weights $A$, commit every matrix other than $j$ honestly, and let $U=\enc(A_j)$. The
committer draws a uniform set $B$ of $e_j+1$ columns. It binds the matrix $E_j$ that equals $U$
outside $B$ and differs from $U$ in every column of $B$, say in its first entry. $E_j$ is not
$e_j$-close. It differs from $U$ on $e_j+1$ columns. A codeword matrix $U'\neq U$ differs from $U$
in some row, hence on at least $n_j-k_j+1$ columns, and so it differs from $E_j$ on at least
$n_j-k_j-e_j>e_j$ columns. In everything else the committer acts as the honest committer of $A$. It
sends the same messages and oracles, and it answers queries to $j$'s tree from $E_j$. Couple this
run with the honest run on $A$ by giving both sides the same random coins. The two runs agree until
the verifier queries a leaf in $B$. In the honest run, the leaves of $j$'s tree that the verifier
queries do not depend on $B$. So all of them, at most $t$ distinct ones, miss $B$ with probability
at least $\binom{n_j-t}{e_j+1}\big/\binom{n_j}{e_j+1}=P_j(t)$. On this event the runs agree
throughout. The verifier then accepts, since by completeness it accepts the honest run.
(ii)~The bound on the number of leaves follows from~(i) and the minimality of $t^0_j(\beta)$. For
$i<t$ and $b=e_j+1$, $b/n_j\le b/(n_j-i)\le b/(n_j-t+1)$, which gives the two bounds on $P_j(t)$.
(iii)~Appendix~\ref{app:untrusted} sets each tree's $t^0$ to the least $t$ with
$P_j(t)\le2^{-\beta_0}$ for all of its members. Our setup proof is complete and column-only, so~(i)
applies to it.
\end{proof}

\para{Merkle trees and Fiat--Shamir.} The argument carries over to a column-only test compiled with
Merkle trees and Fiat--Shamir in the random-oracle model, provided that its verifier uses each root
only to check openings and to derive challenges. The committer of~(i) then builds an honest Merkle
tree over $E_j$. Each challenge is a random-oracle output and, except with negligible probability,
is uniform and independent of $B$. While no opened leaf lies in $B$, every check other than the
Merkle paths is a check that the oracle-model verifier makes on the honest committer's messages for
these challenges. That check passes by completeness. Repeating the attack with fresh commitments
only raises the committer's success. A verifier that recomputes a root from the weights is not
column-only. That is the case $e_j=0$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:untrusted}, which needs no setup proof.

\para{Scope.} The remark is a lower bound for one kind of commitment and one guarantee.
(1)~It covers column-hash commitments only. Some commitments bind each matrix as one long codeword
or as one multilinear polynomial, as the polynomial commitments built on FRI, STIR, WHIR and
BaseFold do. Their proximity proofs open small leaves, and the remark says nothing about them.
Using one would also change the column check of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}. Nor does the remark
cover a commitment that hashes each row separately, whose openings can be single entries.
(2)~It assumes unique decoding, $2e_j<n_j-k_j$. This excludes only the boundary case
$2e_j=n_j-k_j$ that Theorem~\ref{thm:untrusted} admits. For the four models of
Table~\ref{tab:setup} at $a=100$ and $a=1{,}000$, our rule selects radii of at most a quarter of the
unique-decoding radius $(n_j-k_j)/2$. A test of proximity beyond that radius, which FRI-style
analyses allow, need not determine a unique $A^*_j$, which the online part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:untrusted} needs.
(3)~It bounds tests that certify $e_j$-closeness. Under that certificate, the online part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:untrusted} holds for any number of later queries. The committer of~(i) changes only
$e_j+1$ columns, so it stays bound to $A_j$ at radius $e_j+1$. The remark therefore prices the
certificate, not the defence against one particular attack. An analysis that bounds the setup proof
jointly with a bounded number of later queries could accept a test that certifies less. The remark
does not cover such an analysis.
(4)~It holds for every target $\beta$. Our $\beta_0=\lambda+64+\log_2(2|\mathcal J|)$ adds 64 bits
for Fiat--Shamir and $\log_2(2|\mathcal J|)$ bits for a union bound. Any test, interactive or not,
whose error is at most $2^{-\lambda}$ must meet $2^{-\lambda}$ against each committer of~(i), so it
opens at least $t^0_j(\lambda)$ leaves. How many of the further bits a non-interactive test needs
depends on its compilation, for example on a proof of work, and the remark does not settle it.

\para{How much a different test could save.} For Llama-2-7B at $\lambda=128$, with the radii behind
Table~\ref{tab:setup}, the setup proof is 2{,}833.5\,MB at $a=100$. Of this, 2{,}784.0\,MB (98.3\%)
are opened columns, 45.2\,MB (1.6\%) are the combined rows $\psi_jA^*_j$, and at most 4.3\,MB
(0.15\%) are authentication paths; the byte model counts $\log_2n_j$ hashes per opened column. At
$a=1{,}000$ it is 11{,}272.7\,MB: 11{,}210.2\,MB (99.4\%) of columns, the same 45.2\,MB (0.4\%) of
combined rows and at most 17.3\,MB of paths. By Remark~\ref{rem:forced}, a FRI-, STIR-, WHIR- or
BaseFold-style test on this commitment, at the same radii and error per matrix, therefore opens at
least the same columns. It could replace the combined rows with a shorter proof that $\psi_jE_j$ is
close to the code. That saves at most 1.6\% at $a=100$ and 0.4\% at $a=1{,}000$. Everything other
than the columns is 1.7\% and 0.6\% of the setup proof, and at most 3.9\% and 2.3\% for the four
models of Table~\ref{tab:setup}. Now lower the error per matrix from $2^{-\beta_0}$ to $2^{-128}$,
the least that any test of error $2^{-128}$ must meet. The columns alone at the same radii are then
1{,}788.7\,MB at $a=100$ and 7{,}284.7\,MB at $a=1{,}000$. These are 63.1\% and 64.6\% of our setup
proof, and 62.0--64.6\% for the four models. Within the scope of the remark, the columns shrink
below that only with a larger radius, which Theorem~\ref{thm:untrusted} charges to every query
through $k_j+e_j$. For these trees, $\beta_0/{-\log_2(1-e_j/n_j)}$ exceeds $t^0_j$ by 0.9--1.7\% at
$a=100$, and by 3.7--6.9\% at $a=1{,}000$, where $t^0_j$ reaches 12\% of $n_j$.
