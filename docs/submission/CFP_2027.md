# IEEE S&P 2027, second deadline: binding rules and the paper's status

Source: the call for papers, https://sp2027.ieee-security.org/cfpapers.html (read 15 Oct 2026), and the call for
artifacts, https://sp2027.ieee-security.org/cfartifacts.html. The second-deadline HotCRP
(https://cycle2.sp2027.ieee-security.org/) was not yet open. All deadlines are 23:59:59 AoE (UTC-12).

The CFP notes that the second-deadline dates of the early rejection, rebuttal and decision may still be adjusted.
A draft must be on HotCRP 24 hours before the deadline; a server failure in the last 24 hours is no excuse.

## 2. What is fixed at abstract registration (10 Nov)

"Neither the title, abstract, nor the author list can be changed after the abstract registration deadline."
Registration requires a full and complete abstract, every author with an ORCID (whose e-mail and name match
HotCRP), and all PC conflicts. Missing ORCIDs and incorrect or incomplete conflicts lead to desk rejection.
Consequence for the plan: the title, the reframed abstract and its headline numbers must be final on 10 Nov,
two days after the L40S run ends (2-8 Nov).

## 3. Format

- Up to 13 pages of text and up to 5 pages of references and appendices, at most 18 pages in all; everything
  after page 13 must be marked as appendix. Over-length papers are rejected without review. Reviewers need not
  read appendices.
- IEEE "compsoc" conference template, `\documentclass[conference,compsoc]{IEEEtran}`, IEEEtran.cls 1.8b, US
  letter. Changed margins, fonts or line spacing, or "egregious space scrunching", lead to rejection without
  review.

Status of `sp2027-artifact` (1a7ce39, Tectonic build with T1 encoding, Nimbus Roman):

| Rule | Status |
|---|---|
| Class, options, version | `conference,compsoc`, IEEEtran 1.8b: compliant |
| Paper size | 612 x 792 pt (US letter): compliant |
| Spacing changes | no negative `\vspace`, `\linespread`, geometry or float-spacing changes: compliant |
| Anonymous title block | "Anonymous Authors / Submission under double-blind review": compliant |
| 13 pages of text | **3 lines over**: the last sentence of the discussion ends at the top of page 14 |
| 18 pages in all | **19 pages**: page 19 is about 45% full (references 14-16, appendix 16-19) |

Both excesses must be removed before submission, with margin: the final build should be checked with pdfLaTeX
(Overleaf or TeX Live), whose line breaking can differ slightly from XeTeX. The 20 remaining `\todo` boxes
occupy part of the space and disappear once filled, but the cut should not depend on them. The reframe
(`docs/review_20261013/REFRAME.md`, Sec. 5-6) moves the V1 details, the improvement table and the setup proof to
the extended version; that is the room for both the cut and the reframe. Without the reframe, the smallest cut
is the appendix "Soundness of V1" (about 0.75 page) to the extended version, which the body then cites, plus
about ten lines of the body.

## 4. Anonymity and the artifact repository

- No names or affiliations; own work cited in the third person; no identifying acknowledgments; no full CVE
  identifiers.
- Artifact repositories are strongly encouraged and must be anonymous (account, repository name and contents;
  GitFront or Anonymous GitHub are suggested). They "must not be updated after the paper deadline". Theoretical
  papers are encouraged to place their proofs there: the extended version (`main_ext`) belongs in the anonymous
  repository, and `\cite{fullversion}` (extra.bib) must carry its link at submission.
- `code/tools/export_anonymous.py` produces the anonymous tree; it must be uploaded and frozen by 17 Nov.

## 5. HotCRP fields and statements

- **Ethics Considerations** (required at registration). For a new attack the CFP asks for the potential harm of
  publication, whether and how the attack can be mitigated, and the disclosure process. The paper's paragraph
  (`sections/attack.tex`, "Ethics and responsible disclosure") covers the disclosure; the field should also state
  the harm (research protocols, no known deployment) and the mitigation (the defence of Sec. 4).
- **Responsible disclosure.** Disclosure before submission is strongly recommended and is required by the rebuttal
  deadline (16 Feb 2027); the submitted version must describe the steps taken. The notices of 15 Oct (Anchuri et
  al., TensorCommitments) and 19 Oct (CommitLLM, NanoZK, SLP, SPEX) satisfy this if they are sent.
- **Generative AI usage considerations** (required at registration whenever generative AI is used or studied).
  The paper studies the verification of language-model inference, and the code, experiments and parts of the
  text were produced with an LLM agent, so the field must be filled. The CFP asks that use integral to the
  methodology be described explicitly, that ideas from a model be independently validated by the authors, and
  that editorial use be stated as: "Generative AI was used for editorial purposes in this manuscript, and all
  outputs were inspected by the authors to ensure accuracy and originality." The editorial sentence alone does
  not describe agent-written code and experiments. The field should also justify the environmental footprint of
  the experiments (the GPU-hours of the L40S run).
- **Competing interests**: a disclosure of relevant financial interests in the camera-ready version.

## 6. Other rules

- No concurrent submission to a venue with proceedings or a journal; this is assessed at the registration
  deadline. Overlap with the authors' earlier papers must be documented.
- Preprints (arXiv) and talks are allowed during review; results should not be widely advertised, and talks must
  not name the venue.
- At most 6 submissions per author per cycle.
- Rebuttal: 750 words, or 750 words and a revised manuscript for an interactive rebuttal. There are no
  conditional accepts.
- Artifact evaluation is optional and follows acceptance; artifacts must be on a permanent archive with a DOI
  (Zenodo preferred), not GitHub. Badges: available, functional, reproduced.
